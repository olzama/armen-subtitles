# Meme Translation Guide: *The Pokrovsky Gates* (1982), RU → EN

**Setting:** a Moscow communal flat during the Thaw, 1956–57. Most of the humour comes from clashing registers: Margarita's pretentious French against Savva's soldier's German, Soviet officialese against folk wisdom, and lofty phrasing applied to petty situations. Three rules apply throughout:
1. Every callback gets one fixed English wording.
2. Classic quotations use the canonical English source wherever one exists.
3. Each character's register is part of the joke, so keep it.

---

## Tier 1: Catchphrases (fixed wording, keep consistent)

**«Высокие… высокие отношения» (427, 581–582)**
- **Why it's a meme:** a guest uses a reverent, intelligentsia phrase to describe an absurd ménage: the ex-husband, the wife and the new husband dining together. Russians now say it about any tangled romantic or ex-partner arrangement.
- **Effect needed:** deadpan solemnity with a hesitant repeat.
- **Do:** *"Such… such lofty relations."* Avoid "high relations", which suggests drugs. Use the identical wording at 581 so the callback works.

**«Хоботов, это мелко!» (134, 488)**
- **Why:** Margarita's imperious verdict, quoted to shame anyone acting small-minded.
- **Do:** start with the surname and keep it short and cutting: *"Khobotov, that's petty!"* Use the same wording both times.

**«А не хлопнуть ли нам по рюмашке?» → «Заметьте, не я это предложил» (369–371)**
- **Why:** it's the universal alibi for accepting a drink while pretending it wasn't your idea. The joke only works because the speaker is the known drinker, Velyurov (see 360–367).
- **Do:** make the invitation casual (*"How about a quick one?"*) and the reply prim and fussy (*"Mind you, I wasn't the one who suggested it."*).

**«Я вся такая внезапная, такая противоречивая вся» (898–899, 950–951)**
- **Why:** Anna Adamovna's self-dramatising pose, now quoted to mock affected capriciousness. The redundant trailing «вся» is the joke.
- **Do:** keep it clumsy: *"I'm so sudden… so contradictory, all of me."* The 898 version uses «несуразная/угловатая»; translate that variation but keep the sentence frame identical so the repetition is recognisable.

**Savva's German (63, 68, 419, 570, 579, 800, 1025; «гемахт», «полный аллес капут»)**
- **Why:** a veteran applies barrack German to housework. «Натюрлих, Маргарита Пална!» is a meme in its own right, standing for cheerful, total obedience.
- **Do:** leave it in German. English viewers know *Jawohl*, *Achtung* and *kaputt* from war films. Keep the hybrids too: «фюнф минут» becomes *"fünf minutes"* and «полный аллес капут» becomes *"totally alles kaputt"*.
  - «хунд де грабен» is a mangled *da liegt der Hund begraben*. Make its rendering match Savva's own gloss at 570.
  - «Ауфвидеркукен» (579) should stay mangled: *"Auf Wiederkucken"*.
  - Set all of this against Margarita's French (see Tier 2).

**«Резать к чёртовой матери, не дожидаясь перитонитов!»**
- **Why:** a surgeon's brisk verdict, quoted as the motto for any radical "just cut it off" fix. «Единственно прогрессивный взгляд» parodies Party jargon.
- **Do:** be blunt and slightly crude, and keep the comic plural: *"Cut it out, damn it, don't wait for the peritonitises!"* Translate the jargon as *"the only progressive view"*.

**«Я лежал на столе, обнажённый и беззащитный…» / «Чего не надо, того не сделают»**
- **Why:** Khobotov's melodramatic self-pity is deflated by the nurse.
- **Do:** give him a high tragic register (*"naked and defenceless… they could have done anything to me!"*) and her a flat reply (*"They won't do anything you don't need."*).

**«Больные увидят. — Больные не выдадут.» / «у больных большая взаимовыручка»**
- **Why:** an escape plot told in the language of Soviet slogans.
- **Do:** make it sound like a slogan: *"The patients won't give us away. Patients stand by one another."*

**«Живут не для радости, а для совести»**
- **Why:** Savva's folk maxim, quoted half-seriously as a life motto.
- **Do:** keep the balanced aphorism: *"We live not for joy, but for conscience."*

**«История, леденящая кровь! Под маской овцы таился лев!»**
- **Why:** Velyurov's pompous outrage turns "wolf in sheep's clothing" around, and *лев* ("lion") is also Khobotov's first name, Lev.
- **Do:** *"A lion in sheep's clothing!"* keeps the inverted proverb. The name pun is lost. If the subtitles call him "Lev", *"a lion, a Lev!"* recovers some of it.

**Short aphoristic lines (literal translations work; keep them punchy)**
- «Осчастливить против желания нельзя»: *"You can't make someone happy against their will."* This is the film's thesis.
- «Умалишённых не регистрируют!»: *"They don't marry lunatics!"*
- «Неблагодарный эпилептик!»: *"Ungrateful epileptic!"* Keep "epileptic", because it echoes the Dostoevsky line just before it.
- «На всех языках говоришь, а по-русски не понимаешь»: *"You speak every language, but you don't understand plain Russian."*

**Kostik's breakup excuses (1009 ff.) → «Вычеркиваю»**
- **Why:** a running gag in which his invented expeditions get ever more absurd.
- **Do:** fold a brief gloss into the line for the Pushkin reference: *"Prince Oleg's horse's skull, the one with the snake."* Make the payoff crisp and bureaucratic: *"Crossing you off."*

**The dead-poets gag (380–452, 677–681, 1205, 1239)**
- **Why:** literary deaths pile up absurdly («Он что, тоже умер?», «Они все как сговорились»), ending with «Эмиль Золя угорел» → «Выключайте конфорки».
- **Do:** keep the replies short and flat. Keep the real names (Camões, Villon, Rimbaud, Griboyedov). Use *"Zola died of stove fumes" → "Turn off your burners."*

**«Когда я на почте служил ямщиком… имел я селёдку» (1087–1088)**
- **Why:** a drunk garbles a famous romance, turning «силёнку» ("strength") into "herring".
- **Do:** English viewers won't know the song, so the effect to recreate is a bathetic drunken slip. Keep the song frame and swap in a sound-alike: *"…was young, and I had my brawn… my prawn!"*

## Tier 2: Allusions a literal translation would lose

- **Blok vs Kheraskov (826, 838):** the point is "you're not modern, you're antique." Try *"more like some powdered-wig ode-writer."*
- **«мадам баттерфляй» → «Мотылёк!» (846–852):** the swimming-stroke pun works in English as it stands.
- **«по гамбургскому счёту» (1083):** translate the meaning: *"by the strictest standard / no allowances?"*
- **«переквалифицироваться» (1073):** an Ostap Bender echo that can't be carried over. "Retrain" is enough.
- **«Искусство в большом долгу» (472, 1102), «поджигатели войны», «Пой, ласточка, пой» (89–94):** ironic Soviet newspaper-speak. Use English officialese (*"Art is deeply in debt"*, *"warmongers"*).
- **Levitan (104–108):** add a short gloss if needed: "the voice of Soviet radio".
- **Canonical texts:** use published or canonical English versions.
  - Nekrasov (638), Pushkin «Признание» (1290–1303), Lermontov (1253–1255), «Я встретил вас» (1273–1276).
  - Molière (691): *"What the devil was he doing in that galley?"*
  - Matthew 7:1 (1284): KJV *"Judge not…"*
- **Delarue epigram (1376–1377):** this is the punchline to «ещё скажешь мне спасибо» (1375), so the last word must be *"Thank you."*
- **Hamlet:**
  - «тугая плоть» → *"O, that this too too solid flesh would melt…"*
  - «Олень подстреленный» → *"Why, let the strucken deer go weep…"*
  - Kostik's «Тише, это Гамлет» only lands if the line is recognisable, so use Shakespeare's original.
- **Gounod's *Faust*, Mephistopheles' serenade («до обрученья ты не целуй его»):** use the English libretto line or a close paraphrase.
- **«Роза Муда» (460–470):** this is *Rosamunde*, the Beer Barrel Polka. Keep the garbled German as garbled. Savva sings it.
- **«Mon papa ne veut pas…» (480, 1219–1224):** put the real French in the subtitles. Kostik's "translation" should be deliberately dull: *"My father forbade me to dance the polka."*
- **Margarita's French** (*par dépit*, *vous êtes ridicule*, *c'est magnifique*): keep it in French as her class marker, set against Savva's German.
- **Songs:**
  - Velyurov's satirical couplets should become bad rhyming doggerel.
  - «Всё стало вокруг голубым и зелёным» (*Сердца четырёх*, 1941) and «У аптеки» are nostalgic period songs. Translate them for their sense and keep them singable where possible.
- **Finale («Москва молодеет, а я старею…»):** the film's emotional signature, so make it sincere and elegiac with no irony. Gloss Нескучный сад once: *"Neskuchny Garden (the 'Never-Dull Garden')."*

## Tier 3: Wordplay and character lines

- **Injection scene (204–209, 593):**
  - «Вы прекрасно кололи» / «Я ничего не почувствовал» / «Жаль» needs flirtatious double meaning.
  - «уколоться» (593) should keep both senses ("get a shot" and "prick yourself").
- **«Людочка, а не милочка» (506–507):** *"Ludochka, not 'dearie'."*
- **«Шевелюров… менее мануфактурно» / «грассируете»:** Velyurov sounds like *velour*, and Shevelyurov plays on *шевелюра* (a full head of hair). This can't be translated directly, so make an English joke about the fabric sound: *"Velyurov: too much like velour, too upholstered."* «Грассируете» → *"you roll your R's like a Parisian."*
- **«Пора тебя брать!»:** it carries an echo of NKVD arrests. *"Time to take you in"* keeps it.
- **Kostik:** «Константин — постоянный» should be *"Constantine, from the Latin: constant"*, which is ironic for a womaniser. Also «Грядут перемены!», «Спит родимый аквариум», and «из общих ульев в личные гнёзда» (the move from communal flats to private ones).
- **Velyurov:** «Я — Велюров!», «Я попрошу без амикошонства!» (*"No familiarity, please!"*), and «Я служу Мосэстраде» (pompous; gloss as *"the Moscow Variety agency"*).
- **Margarita's pop psychology:** «это кричит твой вакуум», «тайные эротоманы», «Это мой крест». Keep the clinical-sounding jargon.
- **Совранский's «стальной друг»:** this is his motorcycle, described in heroic-Soviet diction. «Догнать Совранского — это утопия!» should stay grandiose.
- **«Ни пуха ни пера — К чёрту»:** *"Break a leg." "Thanks."*
- **Period markers:** «Красная Москва» perfume, «Трудовые резервы», «Физкульт-привет!» (*"Sporting greetings!"*), and the 1957 Youth Festival. Keep the names and gloss them only if they block understanding.

## Loose ends
- **«Заметьте, не я это предложил»:** Chunk 1 cites a source attributing it to Kostik. I attributed it to Velyurov from memory, and that's also the only reading where the joke works. Check the film.
- **Speaker of «Высокие отношения»:** unverified.
- **Hamlet lines:** I believe they're from Pasternak's translation; verify. This doesn't affect the English, which uses Shakespeare.
- **Unverified items:** the A. K. Tolstoy attribution of the Delarue epigram, the songwriters for «Всё стало…» and «У аптеки», the "Как корабль…" poem (645–662), and the Dostoevsky setup before «эпилептик».
- **Probable subtitle source errors:** «идя» → «идея» (1199), «Камойнс» → Camões, «фаллехов гендекоссилаб/тактеля/цесуры», and the garbled polka transliteration.
