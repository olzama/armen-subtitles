=== CHUNK 1 ===
These subtitles are from Leonid Gaidai’s 1973 comedy *Ivan Vasilievich Changes Profession*. Many of its lines are **крылатые фразы**—quotable catchphrases—rather than memes invented on the internet. Some also circulate as image captions and reaction memes. The central translation challenge is the collision of **Soviet bureaucracy, a resourceful burglar, and a sixteenth-century tsar**, often within a single exchange. ([maximonline.ru](https://www.maximonline.ru/entertainment/ivan-vasilevich-menyaet-professiyu-vse-citaty-iz-filma-id6551244/))

The English renderings below are suggestions, not established subtitles. Timestamps refer to the supplied file.

### Particularly recognizable lines

| **13:43–14:27** | **«Это я удачно зашёл!»** — “Well, I picked the right time to drop in!”; **«Граждане, храните деньги в сберегательной кассе! Если, конечно, они у вас есть!»** — “Citizens, keep your money in the savings bank—if you have any, that is.” | Two burglar Miloslavsky catchphrases. The first is gleefully opportunistic; the second borrows the voice of a public-service announcement while he is stealing. Keep the mock-official **“Citizens”** and the deflating final qualification. ([skio.ru](https://skio.ru/afofilms/kino3.php)) |
| **19:35–19:54; 64:44–65:02** | **«Меня терзают смутные сомнения…»** — “I’m beginning to have a nagging suspicion…” | One of the most reusable reaction lines, including as a meme caption. Bunsha politely notices that Miloslavsky has items belonging to robbery victims; the thief later explodes at the implied accusation. *Смутные* means vague, not politically “troubled.” The subtitled **«меня опять…»** is a callback to the earlier suspicion. ([meme-arsenal.com](https://www.meme-arsenal.com/create/meme/13005154?utm_source=openai)) |
| **25:34–26:15** | **«Замуровали! … демоны!»** — “They’ve walled us in! Demons!”; **«Вот что крест животворящий делает!»** — “Behold the power of the life-giving Cross!” | Ivan mistakes the time-machine’s closing wall for demonic work and credits his cross for what follows. His fear and solemn religious language make the technological accident funny. **«Закусывать надо!»** (“You need to eat something with your drink”) is the worldly response to apparently drunken nonsense; *закусывать* means eating food alongside alcohol, not merely “snacking.” ([maximonline.ru](https://www.maximonline.ru/entertainment/ivan-vasilevich-menyaet-professiyu-vse-citaty-iz-filma-id6551244/)) |
| **42:05–43:48** | **«Пусть на обратном пути Казань возьмут. The clerk’s correction—Kazan has **already** been taken—makes the imposture more precarious. Preserve the comically practical “save a trip,” rather than making the order sound strategic. ([maximonline.ru](https://www.maximonline.ru/entertainment/ivan-vasilevich-menyaet-professiyu-vse-citaty-iz-filma-id6551244/)) |

([maximonline.ru](https://www.maximonline.ru/entertainment/ivan-vasilevich-menyaet-professiyu-vse-citaty-iz-filma-id6551244/)) |
| **55:27–56:27** | **«Я. Аз есмь. Житие моё»**; **«Паки, паки… иже херувимы!»** — roughly, “I did. *Az esm’.* My life story”; “*Paki, paki… izhe kheruvimy!*” | Yakin desperately strings together church/archaic-sounding words to speak to Ivan. **It is deliberately incoherent**, not a coherent confession in Old Russian. A translation can retain a few marked sounds, then let the exchange reveal that he does not know the “language”; a fluent biblical-English speech would kill the joke. ([skio.ru](https://skio.ru/afofilms/kino3.php)) |
| **58:52–59:18** | **«Ох, красота-то какая! Лепота!»** — “Oh, what beauty! Splendid!” | Ivan admires modern Moscow. **Лепота** is the distinctive, old-fashioned-sounding punch word and a highly recognizable quotation. “Lovely!” is natural English, but loses the register; “Splendour!” or “What splendour!” may better suggest his voice. ([culture.ru](https://www.culture.ru/themes/531/moskva-v-kino?utm_source=openai)) |
| **61:53–63:12** | **«Кемскую волость… пусть забирают»** — “The Kem district? Let them have it”; **«Такие вопросы … с кондачка не решаются»** — “Questions like that can’t be settled off the cuff.” | The impostor casually gives away territory to a Swedish envoy until the thief intervenes. *Волость* is an administrative district, **not** a “province” or a mere village; *с кондачка* means hastily or without due consideration. The joke also includes Soviet-style deferral—“consult our comrades; drop by next week”—inside a royal audience. ([skio.ru](https://skio.ru/afofilms/kino3.php)) |
| **65:32–65:57; 81:38–81:53** | **«Икра чёрная, красная… заморская … баклажанная!»** — “Black caviar, red caviar—and the exotic imported delicacy… eggplant spread!” | A tiny serving of everyday vegetable *ikra* receives greater ceremony than lavish fish roe. **Баклажанная икра contains no fish eggs**: translate it as “eggplant spread” or “eggplant ‘caviar’,” while retaining “caviar” if possible to preserve the reveal. *Заморская* literally means “from overseas,” ceremoniously “foreign” or “exotic.” The line became shorthand for this comic reversal of luxury and ordinariness. ([vothouse.ru](https://www.vothouse.ru/films/ivan_vasiljevich_menjaet_professiju_quotes.html)) |
| **65:32–65:40; 77:40–77:49** | **«За чей счёт этот банкет?»** — “Who’s paying for this banquet?”; **«Я требую продолжения банкета!»** — “I demand that the banquet continue!” | Both work beyond the film: the first asks who bears the cost of someone else’s extravagance; the second is a comic demand to keep enjoying oneself despite an emergency. In the scene, Bunsha worries about the bill even while pretending to be tsar. ([skio.ru](https://skio.ru/afofilms/kino3.php)) |
| **67:06–67:18** | **«Оставь меня, старушка, я в печали»** — “Leave me be, old woman; I am sorrowful.” | Ivan grandly dismisses Bunsha’s wife as an “old woman.” She immediately objects that she is **five years younger** than her husband. Retain the insult and the pompous self-pity: a neutral “I’m sad; leave me alone” misses both jokes. ([skio.ru](https://skio.ru/afofilms/kino3.php)) |
| **79:23–80:33** | **«Аз есмь царь»** — “I am the tsar”; **«Казань брал, Астрахань брал, Ревель брал, Шпака не брал»** — “I took Kazan, Astrakhan and Reval. I did not take Shpak.” | During a police interview, Ivan answers “Did you take Shpak’s apartment?” as though asked which cities he conquered. Russian *брать* covers both “take/capture” and, colloquially here, burglarize. English may need “take” throughout to make the wordplay audible. **Аз есмь** is emphatically archaic “I am.” ([vothouse.ru](https://www.vothouse.ru/films/ivan_vasiljevich_menjaet_professiju_quotes.html)) |
| **84:45–85:56** | **«Всё, что нажил непосильным трудом…»** — “Everything I earned through backbreaking work…”; **«И тебя вылечат… и меня вылечат!»** — “They’ll cure you too… and me!” | Shpak’s repeated inventory of supposedly hard-earned stolen goods exposes his opportunism. At the police station, Bunsha’s wife cannot tell two identical-looking Ivans apart and finally includes herself among those needing treatment. The escalating **you, you, me** rhythm is the joke; keep it. ([maximonline.ru](https://www.maximonline.ru/entertainment/ivan-vasilevich-menyaet-professiyu-vse-citaty-iz-filma-id6551244/)) |
| **86:13–86:26** | **«Торопиться не надо. Сесть я всегда успею»** — “No need to rush. I’ll have plenty of time to *sit*.” | Miloslavsky is advised to **sit down and write** a doctoral dissertation. For him, *сесть* also means **go to prison**. “I can always get myself locked up later” preserves the intended sense but sacrifices the shared verb; a subtitle might instead use “I can always do time later.” ([vothouse.ru](https://www.vothouse.ru/films/ivan_vasiljevich_menjaet_professiju_quotes.html)) |

### Other passages worth flagging

- **09:05–11:28 — Zina’s departure and «Ты меня пока не выписывай».** Her grave announcement puts stolen gloves beside falling in love, while Shurik is distracted by his machine. She then asks him **not to deregister her from their apartment yet**, in case the new relationship fails. *Выписывать* here concerns residence registration, not a newspaper subscription or a medical discharge. This is more a sustained, culturally specific comic scene than a single top-tier meme. ([maximonline.ru](https://www.maximonline.ru/entertainment/ivan-vasilevich-menyaet-professiyu-vse-citaty-iz-filma-id6551244/))
- **14:30–15:25 — «Это дело общественное» / «соответствующих органов».** Bunsha calls a couple’s divorce “public business” because it damages his building’s performance figures; he also insists that time-travel experiments require approval from the “appropriate authorities.” His bureaucratic register is the point. **Органы** here means state authorities/agencies, with an ominous potential implication—not bodily organs. ([skio.ru](https://skio.ru/afofilms/kino3.php))
- **19:50–20:00 — «Я — артист больших и малых академических театров!»** The burglar evades a request for his name with absurdly inflated theatrical credentials, then says his surname is too famous to give. Translate as a transparent bluff, not a real job title. ([maximonline.ru](https://www.maximonline.ru/entertainment/ivan-vasilevich-menyaet-professiyu-vse-citaty-iz-filma-id6551244/))
- **23:54–24:05 — «Положи трубку!» / «Ложи трубку!»** The shouted repetition includes **ложи**, a nonstandard imperative, in the scuffle over calling the police. A polished, identical “Put down the phone!” each time would erase the change in speech. ([skio.ru](https://skio.ru/afofilms/kino3.php))
- **37:39–38:00 — «Повторяй…»** Miloslavsky means “repeat after me”; Bunsha obediently repeats the **instruction itself** while dictating a royal letter. English can reproduce the gag directly: “Repeat after me…” / “Repeat after me…” / “Don’t repeat *‘repeat after me’!*” ([maximonline.ru](https://www.maximonline.ru/entertainment/ivan-vasilevich-menyaet-professiyu-vse-citaty-iz-filma-id6551244/))
- **39:06–39:18 — «Были демоны… но они самоликвидировались».** Miloslavsky reassures the court that the demons have “eliminated themselves”: a deadpan modern, bureaucratic explanation for an impossible event. **«Шайбу! Шайбу!»** at **41:47** is likewise an intrusion from the present—a hockey crowd’s “Score! Score!” in response to cries for the tsar. ([skio.ru](https://skio.ru/afofilms/kino3.php))
- **50:05–50:23; 56:09–56:16 — «Святой человек со всеми удобствами» / «Красотою лепа…».** Zina’s “saintly man **with all modern conveniences**” comically applies apartment-ad language to her husband. Ivan’s ornate praise of her beauty, by contrast, marks his archaic register. Avoid making either sound like ordinary contemporary romantic speech. ([maximonline.ru](https://www.maximonline.ru/entertainment/ivan-vasilevich-menyaet-professiyu-vse-citaty-iz-filma-id6551244/))
- **61:42–62:00 — «Гитлер капут!»** This is **not a phrase coined by the film**. Unable to answer the envoy’s German, Bunsha offers a scrap of familiar German-sounding wartime language: “Hitler is finished!” Its utter irrelevance is the joke. ([skio.ru](https://skio.ru/afofilms/kino3.php))
- **73:58–74:06; 75:40 — «Нам ли быть в печали?» / «Танцуют все!»** The impostor abruptly turns a royal feast into a modern variety-show party: roughly “Why should *we* be gloomy?” and “Everybody dance!” Keep their buoyant, commanding delivery. ([vothouse.ru](https://www.vothouse.ru/films/ivan_vasiljevich_menjaet_professiju_quotes.html))
- **80:20–80:29 — «Тамбовский волк тебе боярин!»** When Ivan calls the officer *boyarin* (“nobleman”), the officer remodels the established rebuff **«Тамбовский волк тебе товарищ!»** (“A Tambov wolf is your comrade, not me”). “I’m no nobleman of yours!” conveys the refusal, though it loses the recognizable proverbial wolf. ([tambweb.ru](https://tambweb.ru/%D0%A2%D0%90%D0%9C%D0%91%D0%9E%CC%81%D0%92%D0%A1%D0%9A%D0%98%D0%99_%D0%92%D0%9E%D0%9B%D0%9A?utm_source=openai))

### Songs and a borrowed literary line

**«Звенит январская вьюга»** (**06:34–07:59**) and **«Счастье вдруг…»** (**73:53–75:40**) are recognizable songs associated with the film, **not internet-origin jokes**. For the first, Zina appears to sing on screen, but the film vocal is Nina Brodskaya’s; the refrain is often used to identify the song. For the second, also known as **«Разговор со счастьем»**, Mosfilm credits the music to Alexander Zatsepin, lyrics to Leonid Derbenyov and vocal to Valery Zolotukhin. If translating for singing, rhythm matters; for ordinary subtitles, prioritize meaning and mark the lines as lyrics. ([nv.mosconsv.ru](https://nv.mosconsv.ru/sites/default/files/pdf/2023_iv_Zhurkova_.pdf?utm_source=openai))

**«Поговори хоть ты со мной, подруга семиструнная»** (**36:14**) comes from the recording Ivan accidentally plays in Shurik’s apartment; the singer is Vladimir Vysotsky. The “seven-stringed friend” is a **guitar**, not another character speaking. **«Жди меня, и я вернусь»** (**49:23**) is different again: Yakin borrows the opening of Konstantin Simonov’s already-famous wartime poem. Do not attribute that line’s origin to this film. ([culture.ru](https://www.culture.ru/materials/142428/kinopesni-vysockogo?utm_source=openai))

**Overall:** preserve who is speaking *in which century*. Ivan’s “tsar” voice, Bunsha’s administrative clichés, Miloslavsky’s nimble slang, and the misplaced snippets of song or inherited quotation create more of the humour than literal word choice alone.
