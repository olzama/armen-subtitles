## Meme translation guide: *The Diamond Arm*

These lines are chiefly **catchphrases from a widely quoted film**, not necessarily internet memes. In English, their effect often depends on keeping an odd image, repetition, or abrupt change of register rather than making every line sound natural.

| Passage | Why it sticks; what the translation needs |
|---|---|
| **«Шуба подождёт!»** | A brisk decision to put travel before a promised luxury purchase. **“The fur coat can wait!”** keeps both the domestic stakes and the quotable confidence. |
| **«В нашем деле главное — этот самый реализм!»** | Smugglers solemnly demand realism from an absurdly staged accident. Keep the speaker’s fumbling authority: **“In our line of work, the main thing is—uh—realism!”** |
| **«Остров невезения» / «Крокодил не ловится, не растёт кокос»** | The jaunty song makes relentless bad luck funny through its improbable specifics. Keep the crocodile and coconut rather than substituting a generic “nothing goes right”; handle the song’s dated depiction of islanders with care. |
| **«Руссо туристо — облико морале… ферштейн?»** | Mock-foreign speech turns Soviet expectations of a tourist’s moral conduct into a comic performance. Preserve the linguistic jumble, not a polished statement: **“Russo tourist—morals impeccable! Verstehen?”** |
| **«Цигель, цигель, ай-лю-лю!»** | Its urgent, suggestive sound matters more than a lexical meaning. Retain the refrain phonetically and let the scene convey its intent; do not present it as a real foreign-language sentence. |
| **«Чёрт побери!»** | An ordinary exclamation accidentally works as the smugglers’ password. Choose **one** English exclamation—such as “Damn it!”—and repeat it consistently so the mistaken-identity mechanism remains clear. |
| **«Дальше следует непереводимая игра слов…»** | The narrator’s solemn refusal to translate fabricated foreign-sounding argument *is* the joke. Keep the deadpan: **“What follows is untranslatable wordplay involving local idioms.”** |
| **«Поскользнулся, упал… Потерял сознание, очнулся — гипс!»** | A rehearsed cover story becomes funny through its clipped, mechanical repetitions and sudden outcome. **“Slipped. Fell. Blacked out. Woke up—in a cast.”** Preserve any extra details when the character actually says them. |
| **«Я не трус, но я боюсь»** | An endearing contradiction from a man agreeing to help while visibly frightened. **“I’m not a coward—but I am scared.”** Do not make him sound heroic. |
| **«Стамбул — город контрастов»** | A grand lecture title is recycled for a city the lecturer has actually visited. **“Istanbul—a city of contrasts”** should retain its ready-made, slightly empty grandeur. |
| **«Чтоб ты жил на одну зарплату!»** | A crook’s ultimate curse is having no illicit supplementary income. **“May you have to live on your salary alone!”** Let the plain punch line expose his values. |
| **«Собака — друг человека!» / «А у нас управдом — друг человека!»** | The officious building manager appoints herself everyone’s “best friend” in place of a dog. Identify her as a **building manager**, not a mayor or police officer, and keep the presumptuous parallel. |
| **«Детям — мороженое, его бабе — цветы. Смотри, не перепутай!»** | A simple parallel instruction sets up a bungled errand. **“Ice cream for the kids, flowers for his wife. Don’t mix them up!”** Keep it brisk; *баба* is rougher than “wife” if the surrounding dialogue can carry that tone. |
| **«Лёгким движением руки брюки превращаются…»** | A presenter promises an effortless clothing transformation while the mechanism visibly fails. Repeat **“turn into…”** through the struggle: timing and failed confidence make the gag. |
| **«Дёшево, надёжно и практично!» / «Буду бить аккуратно, но сильно!»** | Sales-pitch cheer meets a crude weapon and a contradictory promise. **“Cheap, reliable, practical!”** and **“I’ll hit you carefully—but hard.”** Keep the collisions, not a smoother paraphrase. |
| **«На его месте должен быть я». — «Напьёшься — будешь!»** | A solemn, mistaken declaration of self-sacrifice gets a blunt correction about drunkenness. Keep the exchange together: **“I should be in his place.” — “Get drunk enough and you will be.”** |
| **«Наши люди в булочную на такси не ездят!»** | The building manager casts her disapproval of an extravagant taxi ride as a rule for all respectable Soviet people. **“People like us don’t take taxis to the bakery!”** needs that policing **“us.”** |
| **«Туалэт, типа сортир» / «За чужой счёт пьют даже трезвенники и язвенники»** | The first mixes briefing-room pomposity with a coarse word for toilet; the second turns free drinks into a rhyming cynical maxim. Aim for a similar register clash, then: **“On someone else’s tab, even teetotalers—and ulcer patients—drink.”** |
| **«Кто возьмёт билетов пачку…» / «А если не будут брать — отключим газ!»** | A cheerful lottery pitch ends in a building manager’s casual threat to cut residents’ gas. Preserve the abrupt jump from salesmanship to petty administrative coercion. |
| **«Ты зачем усы сбрил, дурик?» / «Будете у нас на Колыме…» — «Нет уж, лучше вы к нам!»** | A drunk stubbornly mistakes Semён for a moustached friend, then invites him to Kolyma. Translate the moustache question directly; for the invitation, preserve Semён’s conspicuously polite **“No thanks—you’d better visit us!”** Kolyma’s prison-camp association gives that refusal extra force. |
| **«А нам всё равно»** | Frightened hares sing their way into defiant indifference. **“We don’t care!”** works as a repeatable refrain. Keep the playful strangeness of *трынь-трава* without inventing a definite botanical—or secret political—meaning. |
| **«Шампанское по утрам пьют или аристократы, или дегенераты!» / «…какава с чаем»** | Morning champagne receives an absurd two-category verdict; the later bath-and-coffee exchange teases through mangled speech. Preserve the either/or snap of the first line and the deliberately wonky delivery of the second. |
| **«А у вас нет такого же, но с перламутровыми пуговицами?» — «Будем искать!»** | An exquisitely specific request becomes shorthand for wanting an almost identical but elusive alternative. Keep **“one just like it, but with mother-of-pearl buttons?”** and the answering **“We’ll keep looking!”** |
| **«Не виноватая я! Он сам пришёл!»** | A conveniently self-exculpating protest, reusable whenever someone wants to dodge blame. **“It’s not my fault! He came here himself!”** should sound urgent and defensive. |
| **«Шеф, всё пропало! …Гипс снимают, клиент уезжает!»** | A setback becomes a full-scale panic announcement. **“Boss, it’s all gone wrong! They’re taking off the cast; the mark is leaving!”** Here *клиент* is the criminals’ target, not a customer. |
| **«Куй железо, не отходя от кассы!»** | Two familiar formulas are spliced into one nonsensical instruction. **“Strike while the iron’s hot—without leaving the cash register!”** should sound deliberately wrong; an ordinary proverb would lose the meme. |
