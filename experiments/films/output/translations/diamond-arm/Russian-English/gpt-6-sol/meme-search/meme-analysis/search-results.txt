=== CHUNK 1 ===
These subtitles are from Leonid Gaidai’s *The Diamond Arm* (*Бриллиантовая рука*). It is unusually dense with **крылатые фразы**—film lines that people reuse as catchphrases. That does **not** mean every line below began as an internet meme: most became familiar through repeated quotation, and some later acquired meme uses. The film was made in 1968 and reached Soviet cinemas in 1969. ([mosfilm.ru](https://www.mosfilm.ru/cinema/films/brilliantovaya-ruka/?utm_source=openai))

Times below refer to **your subtitles**. The English renderings are suggestions, not established English versions.

| Time | Passage | Why it matters; translation cue |
|---|---|---|
| **06:17–06:23** | **«Шуба подождёт!»** | Semён admits he meant to buy his wife a fur coat instead of travelling; she insists the coat can wait. A quotable way to put an experience ahead of a purchase. **“The fur coat can wait!”** keeps the domestic stakes. ([dslov.ru](https://dslov.ru/ist/ist3.htm)) |
| **06:32–06:59** | **«В нашем деле главное — этот самый реализм!»** | The smugglers insist their staged accident must look authentic, while their own performance is ridiculous. Lёлик’s hesitant, nonstandard delivery is part of the joke: **“In our line of work, the main thing is—uh—realism!”** Mosfilm itself singles the line out as a catchphrase. ([mosfilm.ru](https://www.mosfilm.ru/about/news/brilliantovaya-ruka-a-tolko-li-komediya/)) |
| **08:36–10:13** | **«Остров невезения»**, especially **«Крокодил не ловится, не растёт кокос»** | Gesha’s song about an impossibly unlucky island is a celebrated musical number; its crocodile-and-coconut image can evoke a day when *nothing* goes right. Preserve the absurd specifics rather than replacing them with a generic “bad luck.” The song’s dated portrayal of island “savages” also deserves care in a full translation. ([culture.ru](https://www.culture.ru/persons/8830/andrei-mironov?utm_source=openai)) |
| **12:34–12:37** | **«Руссо туристо — облико морале… ферштейн?»** | One of the film’s best-known lines. Gesha uses mock-foreign words to claim that a Soviet tourist has a proper *moral character* and must refuse the woman’s invitation. It parodies expectations placed on Soviet travellers abroad. **“Russo tourist—morale immaculate! Verstehen?”** conveys the broken-language performance better than polished English does. ([aif.ru](https://aif.ru/culture/movie/neperevodimaya_igra_slov_top-5_samyh_citiruemyh_sovetskih_filmov)) |
| **12:04–12:06; 18:29–18:37** | **«Цигель, цигель, ай-лю-лю!»** | The woman’s pseudo-foreign refrain is memorable chiefly for its sound and urgent, suggestive context. Gesha later glosses *цигель* as “time”; **do not present the whole phrase as a genuine foreign-language sentence**. Keeping its sounds, with the situation supplying the meaning, is a defensible subtitle choice. ([dslov.ru](https://dslov.ru/ist/ist3.htm)) |
| **16:17 onward** | **«Чёрт побери!»** | Literally “Damn it!”—but here the accidental exclamation is also the smugglers’ password. Its many repetitions and foreign-accented imitations drive the mistaken-identity plot. Use **one consistent English exclamation** wherever it functions as the password; varying “damn,” “blast,” and “hell” would obscure the mechanism. ([mosfilm.ru](https://www.mosfilm.ru/cinema/films/brilliantovaya-ruka/?utm_source=openai)) |
| **19:36–19:40** | **«Дальше следует непереводимая игра слов с использованием местных идиоматических выражений»** | The narrator solemnly calls an angry exchange in fabricated foreign speech “untranslatable wordplay.” **The mock translation is itself the joke.** A near-literal subtitle—“What follows is untranslatable wordplay involving local idioms”—preserves that deadpan refusal to translate. ([kinoafisha.info](https://www.kinoafisha.info/movies/5425175/quotes/?utm_source=openai)) |
| **21:15–21:23**, then repeatedly | **«Поскользнулся, упал… Потерял сознание, очнулся — гипс!»** | Semён rehearses a cover story for the cast hiding contraband. It becomes funny through mechanical repetition and is often quoted in shortened form for an implausibly abrupt explanation of an injury. Keep the clipped rhythm and the final reveal: **“Slipped. Fell. Blacked out. Woke up—in a cast.”** Your subtitles also include “closed fracture” in some repetitions; retain it where spoken. ([aif.ru](https://aif.ru/culture/movie/neperevodimaya_igra_slov_top-5_samyh_citiruemyh_sovetskih_filmov)) |
| **26:58–27:08** | **«Я не трус, но я боюсь»** | Semён agrees to serve as bait while plainly frightened. The contradiction makes it affectionate self-deprecation rather than a heroic declaration: **“I’m not a coward—but I am scared.”** ([dslov.ru](https://dslov.ru/ist/ist3.htm)) |
| **29:09–29:20** | **«Стамбул — город контрастов»** | The apartment manager had advertised Semён’s talk as “New York—a city of contrasts,” though he has never been there. She simply substitutes Istanbul: the grand-sounding lecture title matters more than firsthand knowledge. **“Istanbul—a city of contrasts”** should sound like a reusable cliché. ([leonid-gaidai.ru](https://leonid-gaidai.ru/?m=5&r=&s=1&utm_source=openai)) |
| **34:25–34:45** | **«Чтоб ты жил на одну зарплату!»** | Lёlik escalates a string of curses until the supposedly worst fate is **living on one salary**. The joke depends on a crook treating illicit extra income as indispensable. Translate the punch line plainly—**“May you have to live on your salary alone!”**—rather than explaining it inside the subtitle. ([mosfilm.ru](https://www.mosfilm.ru/about/news/brilliantovaya-ruka-a-tolko-li-komediya/)) |
| **35:26–35:39** | **«Собака — друг человека!» / «А у нас управдом — друг человека!»** | Semён invokes the familiar “a dog is man’s best friend” idea; the officious apartment-building manager replaces the dog with herself. **Управдом** is a building or residents’ manager, not a mayor or police officer. The comedy is her claim to be everyone’s compulsory “friend.” ([dslov.ru](https://dslov.ru/ist/ist3.htm)) |
| **36:51–37:43** | **«Детям — мороженое, его бабе — цветы. Смотри, не перепутай!»** | Lёlik’s simple order—ice cream for the children, flowers for Semён’s wife—sets up Gesha’s ineptitude. Keep the brisk parallel form and the warning: **“Ice cream for the kids, flowers for his wife. Don’t mix them up!”** *Баба* is rougher than “wife,” though “his woman” may sound unnaturally harsh in subtitles. ([leonid-gaidai.ru](https://leonid-gaidai.ru/?m=5&r=&s=1)) |
| **39:45–40:03** | **«Лёгким движением руки брюки превращаются…»** | The fashion presenter promises trousers will transform effortlessly into shorts; the mechanism fails while she keeps repeating “transform.” A recognizable line about a supposedly easy transformation that plainly is not easy. **Repeat “turn into…”** as she struggles: the failed timing is the gag. ([leonid-gaidai.ru](https://leonid-gaidai.ru/?m=5&r=&s=1)) |
| **40:59–41:25** | **«Дёшево, надёжно и практично!» / «Буду бить аккуратно, но сильно!»** | Lёlik defends a crude weapon with sales-pitch virtues, then promises a careful *but hard* blow. Both are quoted for their cheerful, self-contradictory practicality. **“Cheap, reliable, practical”** and **“I’ll hit you carefully—but hard”** keep the collisions intact. ([leonid-gaidai.ru](https://leonid-gaidai.ru/?m=5&r=&s=1)) |
| **42:14–42:21** | **«На его месте должен быть я». — «Напьёшься — будешь!»** | Semён mistakes an incapacitated drunk for a corpse and makes a solemn, self-sacrificing remark; the reply means, roughly, **“Get drunk enough and you will be.”** The second speaker punctures an imagined heroic scene with the prosaic explanation. Keep both halves of the exchange. ([aif.ru](https://aif.ru/culture/movie/neperevodimaya_igra_slov_top-5_samyh_citiruemyh_sovetskih_filmov)) |
| **43:22–43:25** | **«Наши люди в булочную на такси не ездят!»** | Perhaps the clearest standalone catchphrase here. The manager finds a taxi trip to a bakery suspiciously extravagant; **наши люди** (“people like us”) turns her personal judgment into a claim about proper Soviet behaviour. **“People like us don’t take taxis to the bakery!”** preserves that policing tone. It is documented both as a film catchphrase and in later everyday use. ([ls.pushkininstitute.ru](https://ls.pushkininstitute.ru/storage/kino/brilruk_af_1/?utm_source=openai)) |
| **50:25–50:38; 51:22–51:27** | **«Туалэт, типа сортир»; «За чужой счёт пьют даже трезвенники и язвенники»** | Lёlik mixes pompous operational briefing with coarse, redundant description of a toilet; later he offers a cynical maxim for getting Semён drunk. For the latter, **“On someone else’s tab, even teetotalers—and ulcer patients—drink”** preserves both the free-drinks joke and the neat pairing *трезвенники / язвенники*. ([dslov.ru](https://dslov.ru/ist/ist3.htm)) |
| **51:48–52:07** | **«Кто возьмёт билетов пачку…» / «А если не будут брать — отключим газ!»** | A lottery-ticket sales pitch turns into the building manager’s blithe threat to cut residents’ gas if they refuse to buy. The payoff is her abuse of petty administrative power, not a sincere public-service announcement. ([leonid-gaidai.ru](https://leonid-gaidai.ru/?m=5&r=&s=1)) |
| **58:28–59:34** | **«Ты зачем усы сбрил, дурик?»; «Будете у нас на Колыме — милости просим!» — «Нет уж, лучше вы к нам!»** | A drunk stranger insists Semён is an old friend who has shaved off his moustache; later he warmly invites him to Kolyma. Semён’s **“No thanks—you’d better come visit us!”** lands as a polite refusal made emphatic by Kolyma’s association with Soviet prison camps. Translate the moustache question directly: its stubborn mistaken identity is the joke. ([leonid-gaidai.ru](https://leonid-gaidai.ru/?m=5&r=&s=1)) |
| **01:02:02 onward** | **«А нам всё равно»**, the refrain of **«Песня про зайцев»** | Semён’s drunken song about frightened hares asserting that they do not care has outlived the scene as a recognizable expression of defiant indifference. **“We don’t care!”** works for the refrain. *Трынь-трава* is not a botanical species that needs identifying: its playful sound connects the hares’ “magic grass” to the sense that troubles are trifles. Avoid unsupported claims that the song has one secret political or drug-related meaning. ([song-story.com](https://song-story.com/pesnya-pro-zaycev/?utm_source=openai)) |
| **01:07:29–01:08:09** | **«Шампанское по утрам пьют или аристократы, или дегенераты!»; «…ванну… кофе… какава с чаем»** | Lёlik labels Gesha’s morning champagne either aristocratic or degenerate. Gesha’s elaborate request for a bath and coffee is then answered with mock reassurance that there will be a bath, coffee, and mangled **“cocoa with tea.”** Preserve the champagne line’s absurd two-option verdict; preserve the second exchange’s slurred, teasing delivery. ([dslov.ru](https://dslov.ru/ist/ist3.htm)) |
| **01:12:07–01:13:00** | **«А у вас нет такого же, но с перламутровыми пуговицами?» — «Будем искать!»** | Semён’s oddly specific shopping request becomes the pretext for luring him to a hotel. Outside the film, “the same thing, but with mother-of-pearl buttons” is recognizable shorthand for wanting an elusive, near-identical version of something. **“Do you have one just like it, but with mother-of-pearl buttons?”** is worth keeping exact; “We’ll keep looking” completes the exchange. ([russianlife.com](https://www.russianlife.com/uchites/uchites-55-mar-apr-2019/?utm_source=openai)) |
| **01:22:00–01:22:06** | **«Не виноватая я! Он сам пришёл!»** | The woman caught in the compromising hotel-room setup protests that he came of his own accord. The line is reused as a transparently convenient denial of responsibility and **has documented internet-meme adaptations**. **“It’s not my fault! He came here himself!”** retains the defensive insistence. ([pressto.amu.edu.pl](https://pressto.amu.edu.pl/index.php/strp/article/download/34364/29750)) |
| **01:25:32–01:25:40** | **«Шеф, всё пропало! …Гипс снимают, клиент уезжает!»** | Gesha’s frantic report that their plan is collapsing is a ready-made line for melodramatic panic over a setback. **“Boss, it’s all gone wrong! They’re taking off the cast; the mark is leaving!”** Here *клиент* is the criminals’ target, not a paying customer. ([dslov.ru](https://dslov.ru/ist/ist3.htm)) |
| **01:28:49–01:29:02** | **«Куй железо, не отходя от кассы!»** | A comic mash-up: “strike while the iron is hot” meets the instruction to check money **before leaving the cashier’s desk**. **“Strike while the iron’s hot—without leaving the cash register!”** deliberately sounds wrong; smoothing it into an ordinary English proverb would erase the joke. ([livespeak.academic.ru](https://livespeak.academic.ru/829/%D0%9A%D1%83%D0%B9_%D0%B6%D0%B5%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%B7%D0%BE%2C_%D0%BD%D0%B5_%D0%BE%D1%82%D1%85%D0%BE%D0%B4%D1%8F_%D0%BE%D1%82_%D0%BA%D0%B0%D1%81%D1%81%D1%8B?utm_source=openai)) |

**Translation priority:** preserve the film’s *recognizable oddness*—repeated passwords, broken pseudo-foreign speech, pompous Soviet officialese, literal-minded replies, and proverbs that do not quite fit together. An elegant idiomatic English line can be less faithful here than a slightly strange one. ([mosfilm.ru](https://www.mosfilm.ru/about/news/brilliantovaya-ruka-a-tolko-li-komediya/))
