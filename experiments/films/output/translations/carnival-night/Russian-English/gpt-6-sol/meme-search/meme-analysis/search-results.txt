=== CHUNK 1 ===
These subtitles are from **Eldar Ryazanov’s *Carnival Night* (*Карнавальная ночь*, 1956)**. Its most recognizable lines are not all internet memes in the narrow sense: several are *крылатые фразы*—film quotations reused as jokes in ordinary speech—while the songs have a separate life as New Year standards. The central comic contrast is between a staff trying to put on a party and acting director Ogurtsov, who tries to administer the fun out of it. ([mosfilm.ru](https://www.mosfilm.ru/cinema/films/karnavalnaya-noch/?utm_source=openai))

| Subtitle time | Passage and cultural context | What to preserve in English |
|---|---|---|
| **03:19** | **«Есть установка весело встретить Новый год»** (“We have a directive to celebrate New Year cheerfully”). A well-known Ogurtsov line: he makes *having fun* sound like compliance with an official order. A film essay even uses it as its title. ([kinoart.ru](https://www.kinoart.ru/texts/est-ustanovka-veselo-vstretit-novyy-god-chudesa-i-mechty-v-sovetskom-kino?utm_source=openai)) | **Установка** is an instruction from above, not a personal intention. “We’ve been instructed to have a good time” carries the contradiction. |
| **04:35; repeated at 35:28** | **«Коротенько, минут на сорок»** (“Just briefly—about forty minutes”). This became a stock ironic quotation for a supposedly short speech that will be anything but. Ogurtsov first proposes it for the party’s opening address, then for a lecture. ([diagram.com.ua](https://www.diagram.com.ua/idioms/idioms1246.shtml?utm_source=openai)) | Keep *briefly* beside **forty minutes**; that collision is the joke. |
| **05:14–05:27** | **«Котов урезать до минимума» / «Бабу Ягу … воспитаем в своём коллективе»**. Faced with a costume budget, Ogurtsov proposes cutting the number of Pusses-in-Boots and “raising” a Baba Yaga internally. The Baba Yaga line is especially portable: it has been reused in discussions of recruiting or promoting staff. ([kz.kinoafisha.info](https://kz.kinoafisha.info/articles/babu-yagu-so-storony-brat-ne-budem-vospitaem-v-svoem-kollektive-8-umoritelnyh-citat-iz-karnavalnoy-nochi_id91448_a15745825/?utm_source=openai)) | These are fairy-tale *characters* being treated as budgeted personnel. “We won’t hire a Baba Yaga from outside; we’ll train one in-house” retains both the folklore and HR jargon. |
| **14:11–14:24** | **«Добавьте сюда ещё людей, будет большой, массовый квартет»** (“Add more people and you’ll have a big, mass-participation quartet”). Another frequently collected Ogurtsov quotation: his desire for an impressive, large-scale production overrides what *quartet* means. ([quote-citation.com](https://quote-citation.com/topic/karnavalnaya-noch?utm_source=openai)) | Keep **quartet**, not just “group.” The impossible “large quartet” is the punchline. |
| **16:21–16:41** | **«Чтобы [музыка] тебя вела, но … не уводила»** (“The music should lead you—but not lead you away”). Ogurtsov sounds authoritative while giving the musicians no usable artistic direction; the line appears in film-quotation collections. ([quote-citation.com](https://quote-citation.com/topic/karnavalnaya-noch?utm_source=openai)) | Preserve the repetition of **lead**, rather than replacing it with sensible musical criticism. |
| **20:35** | **«Нам Гоголи-Щедрины нужны!»** (“We need Gogols and Shchedrins!”). Ogurtsov invokes two canonical satirists while trying to dictate what acceptable satire should look like. The formulation echoes an earlier Soviet political pronouncement, making his appeal to satire more pointed than a casual literary reference. ([intelros.ru](https://www.intelros.ru/readroom/nlo/121-2013/19673-gogoli-i-schedriny-uroki-polozhitelnoy-satiry.html?utm_source=openai)) | Keep the writers’ names. A brief note can identify Gogol and Saltykov-Shchedrin as satirists; don’t turn the line into the generic “We need comedians.” |
| **38:45–42:34** | **«Пять минут»** (“Five Minutes”). This is **a New Year song, not principally a meme**: Gurchenko’s performance became one of the film’s lasting cultural signatures and a traditional holiday song. The five minutes before midnight are also a chance to reconcile or say something one has put off. ([culture.ru](https://www.culture.ru/materials/163183/ot-shelkunchika-do-charodeev-7-melodii-o-novom-gode?utm_source=openai)) | Retain the countdown to midnight and the urgency of saying things *before the year ends*. English subtitles need not reproduce every rhyme to convey why this number matters. |
| **43:19** | **«Ловкость рук и никакого доклада!»** (“Sleight of hand—and no speech!”). The magician makes Ogurtsov’s prepared address disappear. It also plays on an older familiar magician’s formula, **«Ловкость рук — и никакого мошенства»** (“Sleight of hand—and no cheating”): *доклада* (“speech/report”) replaces *мошенства*. ([old.vspu.ru](https://old.vspu.ru/sites/default/files/disfiles/dissertations/dissertaciya_kazakovoy_a.i._0.pdf?utm_source=openai)) | “Sleight of hand—and no speech!” is clear in the scene. If an annotation is possible, mention the altered catchphrase; a subtitle alone need not force an English catchphrase that does not exist. |
| **45:52–46:39** | **«Лучше всего, конечно, пять звёздочек!» / «Есть ли жизнь на Марсе … это науке неизвестно»**. The drunken lecturer shifts from telescope “stars” to **five-star cognac**, then concludes that science cannot say whether there is life on Mars. The Mars line became a standalone quotation, often used for a comically noncommittal answer; the Pushkin Institute explicitly teaches both the quotation and the cognac joke. ([ls.pushkininstitute.ru](https://ls.pushkininstitute.ru/storage/kino/karnoch_af_1/?utm_source=openai)) | Make the double meaning of **stars** available to viewers—five stars can rate a bottle, not merely appear through a telescope. Deliver “science doesn’t know” as his anticlimax, **not** as the film’s serious scientific claim. |
| **48:53–49:05** | **«Ансамбль пенсии и пляски»**, immediately corrected to **«ансамбль песни и пляски»**. Grisha accidentally announces a “pension-and-dance ensemble” instead of a “song-and-dance ensemble,” because the performers are retirees. ([mosfilm.ru](https://www.mosfilm.ru/cinema/films/karnavalnaya-noch/?utm_source=openai)) | “The pension-and-dance ensemble—sorry, **song**-and-dance ensemble” makes the slip audible. This is a scene-specific pun, rather than one of the film’s biggest standalone catchphrases. |
| **1:00:15–1:01:22** | The fable **«Медведь на балу»** (“The Bear at the Ball”) depicts a bear who ruins a joyful gathering by questioning and controlling everything. Ogurtsov praises its “bite” yet asks who the bear is; told it is an allegory, he advises naming the person and workplace next time. ([mosfilm.ru](https://www.mosfilm.ru/cinema/films/karnavalnaya-noch/?utm_source=openai)) | Keep the fable’s indirect accusation and Ogurtsov’s failure to recognize himself. His demand for a **name and workplace** is the bureaucratic final joke. |
| **1:05:13–1:07:37** | **«И хорошее настроение не покинет больше вас»** (“And your good mood will never leave you”). This refrain belongs to *Песенка о хорошем настроении* (“Song of Good Cheer”), another widely remembered song from the film. Unlike Ogurtsov’s commands, its optimism is meant sincerely. ([kinoafisha.info](https://www.kinoafisha.info/articles/tayna-lyubimoy-pesni-iz-karnavalnoy-nochi-poyavilas-v-filme-neobychnym-obrazom_id69034_a14813648/?utm_source=openai)) | Avoid giving the song Ogurtsov’s sarcastic tone. It expresses the film’s answer to his compulsory “fun.” |

**One translation-wide caution:** the supplied subtitles contain spelling errors and inconsistent transcriptions. In particular, Ogurtsov’s recurring **«сурьёзно»** is a nonstandard pronunciation of *серьёзно* (“seriously”). His solemn bureaucratic diction is essential to the humour, but reproducing every misspelling as broken English would mischaracterize the film. ([uchees.ru](https://www.uchees.ru/citaty/Karnavalnaja-noch-1256.html?utm_source=openai))
