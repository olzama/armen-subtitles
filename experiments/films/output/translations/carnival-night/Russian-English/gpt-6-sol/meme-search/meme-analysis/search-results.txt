=== CHUNK 1 ===
The subtitles are from Eldar Ryazanov’s **1956 film *Carnival Night* (*Карнавальная ночь*)**. Many of its lines are *крылатые фразы*—quotable catchphrases—rather than internet memes in the narrower sense. The running joke is that acting director Ogurtsov tries to make a New Year’s carnival orderly, morally instructive and, above all, **“сурьёзно”**: his conspicuously nonstandard pronunciation of “seriously.” ([mosfilm.ru](https://www.mosfilm.ru/cinema/films/karnavalnaya-noch/?utm_source=openai))

| Time | Passage and status | What an English translator should catch |
|---|---|---|
| **03:19–04:02** | **«Есть установка весело встретить Новый год»**; **«Я и сам шутить не люблю и людям не дам!»** Both are established film catchphrases. ([diletant.media](https://diletant.media/articles/38645111/?utm_source=openai)) | *Установка* is an instruction from above: “We’ve been directed to have a good time this New Year.” The contradiction is the joke. The second line needs its deadpan authoritarian turn: “I don’t like joking myself, and I won’t let anyone else joke either.” |
| **04:35–04:46** | **«И так коротенько, минут на сорок…»**—the “short” speech that will take forty minutes. One of the film’s best-known lines. ([diletant.media](https://diletant.media/articles/38645111/?utm_source=openai)) | Keep *short* and *forty minutes* together: “Just a brief speech—forty minutes or so.” It captures Ogurtsov’s obliviousness better than a literal rendering of *даёт своё выступление*. |
| **05:18–05:28** | **«Бабу Ягу со стороны брать не будем, воспитаем в своём коллективе!»** A particularly durable quote, also used jokingly about producing one’s own troublesome person. ([libking.ru](https://libking.ru/books/ref-/reference/1100147-5-konstantin-dushenko-nastolnyj-slovar-citat.html?utm_source=openai)) | Baba Yaga is the witch in Russian folklore; *со стороны* means recruiting an outsider, while *воспитаем в коллективе* sounds like earnest workplace policy. “No need to hire a Baba Yaga from outside—we’ll cultivate one in-house” retains both halves of the joke. |
| **12:31–12:40; 14:13–14:25** | **«Костюмы надо заменить, ноги изолировать!»** and **«…будет большой, массовый квартет»** are both listed among the film’s catchphrases. ([stanislaw.ru](https://stanislaw.ru/rus/movies/card.asp?id=carnavalnoch&utm_source=openai)) | “Isolate the legs” makes dancers’ bare legs sound like a technical hazard; consider “The costumes must go; the legs must be covered up!” A **“large, mass-participation quartet”** should remain a contradiction: Ogurtsov has not grasped that a quartet has four performers. |
| **20:54–21:20** | **«Я одна, совсем одна… с моим здоровым коллективом!»** appears in collections of the film’s quotable lines. ([diletant.media](https://diletant.media/articles/38645111/?utm_source=openai)) | He “corrects” a wistful song about being alone by replacing its intimate ending with a reference to her work collective. *Здоровым* suggests a wholesome or healthy collective. The humour is his inability to leave a lyric alone. |
| **42:59–43:24** | **«Ловкость рук и никакого доклада!»** is a film-specific twist on an older magician’s saying, “sleight of hand and no trickery.” A linguistic study explicitly notes the substitution of *доклад* (“speech/report”). ([vestnik.sutr.ru](https://vestnik.sutr.ru/journals_n/1383374157.pdf?utm_source=openai)) | The magician has made Ogurtsov’s planned speech disappear. “Sleight of hand—and no speech!” conveys the immediate gag; reproducing the older Russian catchphrase’s echo would require a translator’s note. |
| **45:52–46:39** | **«Лучше всего, конечно, пять звёздочек!»**; **«Есть ли жизнь на Марсе, нет ли жизни на Марсе — это науке неизвестно»**. The Mars line is one of the film’s most widely repeated quotations. ([diletant.media](https://diletant.media/articles/38645111/?utm_source=openai)) | The tipsy lecturer is ostensibly counting stars through a telescope, but **five stars** also evoke the rating of cognac. His grand answer—“Whether there’s life on Mars or not, science doesn’t know”—delivers virtually no information. Preserve the repeated, circular phrasing rather than polishing it into a scientific statement. ([kommersant.ru](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/4499762?utm_source=openai)) |
| **48:53–49:04** | **«Ансамбль пенсии и пляски… ансамбль песни и пляски!»** A remembered verbal slip from Grisha’s nervous debut as compère. ([kinomoment.ru](https://kinomoment.ru/moment/4412/?utm_source=openai)) | *Песни и пляски* is “song and dance”; *пенсии* turns “song” into “pension,” apt for the elderly performers. “The Pension-and-Dance Ensemble—sorry, the Song-and-Dance Ensemble” keeps the slip audible. |
| **59:27–1:01:22** | The fable **«Медведь на балу»** ends by asking why the bear came to the ball; Ogurtsov then asks who the bear is and requests a **surname and workplace** next time. The exchange is also singled out in film-quote collections. ([stanislaw.ru](https://stanislaw.ru/rus/movies/card.asp?id=carnavalnoch&utm_source=openai)) | The bear is an allegory for the meddling Ogurtsov, which everyone understands except him. His demand for identification destroys the point of a fable. Don’t present this as an actual Krylov fable: in the film, the accountant performs it to satirize his boss. |
| **1:12:07–end** | **«За всё, что здесь сегодня было, я лично никакой ответственности не несу!»** is the film’s final, frequently quoted declaration. ([stanislaw.ru](https://stanislaw.ru/rus/movies/card.asp?id=carnavalnoch&utm_source=openai)) | “I personally take no responsibility for anything that happened here tonight!” is an apt final line for a manager who tried to control everything, then disowns the successful celebration. |

**Two songs deserve separate treatment as cultural touchstones, not primarily memes.** At **38:45–42:34**, Lyudmila Gurchenko’s **«Пять минут»** (“Five Minutes”) counts down to midnight and remains a traditional New Year’s song; *без пяти двенадцать* means **11:55**. At **1:05:13–1:07:37**, **«Песенка о хорошем настроении»** (“Song of Good Cheer”) is another independently famous number—its refrain about a smile and good spirits is recognisable beyond this scene. For subtitles, prioritise singable rhythm if the songs will be performed in English; otherwise make the countdown and the reassuring refrain immediately clear. ([culture.ru](https://www.culture.ru/materials/163183/ot-shelkunchika-do-charodeev-7-melodii-o-novom-gode?utm_source=openai))

A smaller pragmatic joke occurs at **1:10:19**: someone cries **«Горько!»**, the customary call for a couple to kiss at a Russian wedding, then pivots to “It’s bitter to feel we’re a year older.” A bare “Bitter!” will puzzle English viewers; the subtitle needs to signal the wedding-call expectation before the reversal.
