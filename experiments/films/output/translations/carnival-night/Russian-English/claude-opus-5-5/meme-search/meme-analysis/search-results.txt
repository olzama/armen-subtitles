=== CHUNK 1 ===
This is **«Карнавальная ночь»** (*Carnival Night*, 1956). Eldar Ryazanov directed it, from a script by Boris Laskin and Vladimir Polyakov. Igor Ilyinsky plays Ogurtsov and Lyudmila Gurchenko plays Lena. It's one of the most quoted Soviet comedies and is shown on TV every New Year. Most of the quotes come from Ogurtsov's bureaucratic jargon.

I ran three searches and read one page. The web confirmed only some of the links below. The rest are my own background knowledge and are marked **(unverified)**.

## Confirmed catchphrases (listed on stuki-druki)

| # | Line | What a translator should know |
|---|---|---|
| 20 | *Есть установка весело встретить Новый год* | *Установка* means an official directive from above. The joke is fun issued as an order. Something like "We have a directive to see in the New Year cheerfully." |
| 18 | *Зачем нашим советским людям скрывать своё лицо?* | He objects to masks at a masquerade. The "Soviet people have nothing to hide" logic is the joke. |
| 25, 127, 286, 388 | ***сурьёзно / сурьёз*** | His signature mispronunciation of *серьёзно*. It's a running gag and he's quoted with it, so keep it visibly nonstandard ("serious-like", "suh-rious"). Standard "serious" loses the character. |
| 27 | *Я и сам шутить не люблю и людям не дам* | Ilyinsky says *людЯм* with the wrong stress; the subtitle doesn't show this. Today it's used ironically about humourless bosses. |
| 35, 309 | *коротенько, минут на сорок* | Still used about long-winded speakers. Keep the contrast between "short" and "forty minutes". |
| 43–44 | ***Бабу Ягу со стороны брать не будем, воспитаем в своём коллективе*** | A huge meme, used about promoting from within (often ironically about bad internal hires). *Единица* means a budgeted staff position. Something like: "No outside hire for Baba Yaga — we'll grow our own." Keep "Baba Yaga"; English-speaking viewers know her. The joke just before it, "cut the cats" (Puss in Boots is budgeted but the boots aren't), is a budgeting joke and should stay literal. |
| 100–102 | *Его голыми ногами не воспитаешь… ноги изолировать* | "Isolate the legs" is technical or quarantine language applied to dancers' bare legs. Keep it stiff and odd. |
| 116–117 | ***большой, массовый квартет*** | A standard Russian example of an absurd contradiction, mocking the Soviet love of huge mass events. "A big, mass quartet" works literally. |
| 123–126 | *Даёт она что? … Нужно, чтоб музыка брала… вела, но не уводила* | Empty official vagueness. *Уводила* (lead astray) hints at ideological deviation. |
| 159 | ***Нам Гоголи-Щедрины нужны*** | **(unverified)** I believe this mocks Malenkov's 1952 Party Congress line "нам нужны советские Гоголи и Щедрины" ("we need Soviet Gogols and Shchedrins"), which was widely ridiculed. An English viewer won't know Saltykov-Shchedrin. A short gloss or "Gogols and Swifts" are options. |

## Other famous or culturally loaded passages

- **393–399, the drunk lecturer's Mars talk** (Sergei Filippov; the speaker is the lecturer, not Ogurtsov). *Есть ли жизнь на Марсе, нет ли жизни на Марсе — это науке неизвестно* is the best-known line in the film. It's still quoted whenever anything is uncertain, and the search showed people citing it on Habr and in KP.
  - **(unverified)** The stars slide from "two, three, four" to *лучше всего — пять звёздочек*: brandy is graded by stars on the label, and he's drunk. This translates well ("five-star is best, of course").
  - *Вооружённым глазом* flips *невооружённым глазом* ("with the naked eye"), so "with the *armed* eye" or "with the clothed eye".
  - *Наука ещё пока не в курсе дела* puts bureaucratic language into science.
  - *Асса!* is a Georgian/Caucasian dance cry.
- **307, *лектор из общества по распространению*** **(unverified):** the full name was "Society for the Dissemination of Political and Scientific Knowledge" (later *Знание*). The truncation is the joke: "from the Society for the Dissemination".
- **94, *«Медведи на отдыхе»*:** a philistine name for a kitschy picture, almost certainly pointing to Shishkin's bears (my reading). It sets up the bear fable at 466–477, where the bear is Ogurtsov. *По перечислению* means "by bank transfer", which is bureaucratic, not about art.
- **127, *А и Б играли на трубе*:** he garbles a children's riddle (*А и Б сидели на трубе…*). The garbling shows he can't even get the nursery rhyme right. There's no exact English equivalent; "Pete and Repeat" is the nearest.
- **164 / 216, *типично / нетипично*** **(unverified):** this mocks the Stalin-era doctrine of "the typical" in art, which I believe was also in Malenkov's speech. "Typical" is a loaded word here, so keep it consistent every time it appears.
- **223–224, *вся соль! — А при чём тут соль?*:** a pun on "the salt" meaning "the point". Something like "That's the punchline! — What punch?"
- **233 → 426, *петрушка*:** "a muddle", and also Petrushka, the Russian Punch puppet. At 426 he says *из директора Петрушку делают*. Use one image for both, e.g. "a Punch-and-Judy show" … "making a Punch out of the director".
- **236, *морально разложился*:** stock Soviet language for moral decay, used deadpan on a clown routine.
- **376, *Ловкость рук и никакого доклада*:** plays on the magician's patter *ловкость рук и никакого мошенничества*. Something like "Sleight of hand — and no report!"
- **420, *Ансамбль пенсии и пляски*:** a slip on *песни и пляски* (the "Song and Dance Ensemble" was a standard Soviet format). English needs a matching pun, e.g. "Pension and Dance Ensemble".
- **422–423:** "composer Kruchini" is fake Italian built on *кручина* ("grief"; my reading), and the conductor's double-barrelled name is a comic invention.
- **456 / 530, *Буду жаловаться в ВЦСПС*:** the all-union trade-union council, the standard place to complain to. It needs a gloss or a substitute.
- **477 / 486, *Зачем на бал пришёл медведь?* … *просто называй фамилию и место работы*:** Telegin's request is a well-known joke about writers hiding criticism in fables. Keep it literal.
- **463, *Зайдите попозже, кассир уехал в банк*:** "Come back later, the cashier's gone to the bank", the typical office brush-off.
- **543–544, *нашли в моём лице дурака*:** *в моём лице* is official language, and the secretary repeating it back is the joke.
- **546–547, *Горько!*:** the wedding toast that makes a couple kiss, turned into "it's bitter to be a year older". It needs explaining, or a rebuild around the kiss.
- **561, the closing line *Официально заявляю… никакой ответственности не несу*:** spoken to the camera and widely quoted as a disclaimer. Keep it formal.
- **Songs:** «Песенка о пяти минутах» (343–362) and «Песенка о хорошем настроении» (511–524) are both very well known. The first is a New Year's Eve standard. *Без пяти минут мастер* (358) is an idiom for "almost a master", and the song plays on it. Song lines are fixed cultural texts, so translate them for meaning; there's no established English version to match.

- In 525/528 the surname appears as both "Бурыкина" and "Буракина".
