# Meme Translation Guide: *Carnival Night* (1956), RU → EN

**General principle:** Most of the memes come from Ogurtsov applying Soviet bureaucratic language to fun, art and holidays. The English needs a consistent **officialese register**: directive, collective, staff position, measures. Plain natural English removes the joke. Wherever a line has a meme afterlife, it should still work as a standalone quotable sentence.

---

### 18. *Зачем нашим советским людям скрывать своё лицо?*
- **Why it's a meme:** He reads a masquerade as a political problem. Hiding your face implies you have something to hide.
- **What matters:** The suspicion logic, and the solemn word *советским*.
- **Translator:** Keep "Soviet" and keep the rhetorical question: *"Why should our Soviet people hide their faces?"* Don't soften it to "Why masks?"

### 20. *Есть установка весело встретить Новый год*
- **Why it's a meme:** Joy issued as an order from above. People still quote it whenever fun is mandated by management.
- **Translator:** Use a word that sounds like it came down from the top: *"We have a directive to see in the New Year cheerfully."* "Plan" or "idea" is too weak.

### 25, 127, 286, 388. *сурьёзно / сурьёз*
- **Why it's a meme:** It's his verbal signature, the uneducated boss who sounds pompous. Russians quote it with the mispronunciation.
- **Translator:** Use a visibly wrong spelling, and the same one at all four points (*"seerious," "serus"*). A correct "serious" erases the character.

### 27. *Я и сам шутить не люблю и людям не дам*
- **Why it's a meme:** A humourless man openly bans humour in a comedy. People use it ironically about joyless bosses.
- **Translator:** Keep the blunt two-part symmetry: *"I don't like jokes myself, and I won't let others joke either."* The stress error (*людя́м*) can't be subtitled, so let it go.

### 35, 309. *коротенько, минут на сорок*
- **Why it's a meme:** It's the standard Russian jab at long-winded speakers.
- **Translator:** The diminutive *коротенько* needs a matching cosy word before the number lands: *"Just a short one, forty minutes or so."* The number has to come last.

### 43–44. *Бабу Ягу со стороны брать не будем, воспитаем в своём коллективе*
- **Why it's a meme:** It's one of the best-known Soviet catchphrases. People use it about promoting from within, especially when the internal candidate is the "witch".
- **What matters:** The HR language (*со стороны*, *коллектив*, *единица*) applied to a fairy-tale witch.
- **Translator:** Keep "Baba Yaga" and keep "collective": *"We won't hire a Baba Yaga from outside. We'll raise our own, in our collective."* Keep the preceding Puss-in-Boots budgeting joke literal (the cat is budgeted, the boots aren't), because it sets up the same staffing logic.

### 100–102. *голыми ногами не воспитаешь… ноги изолировать*
- **Why it's a meme:** Bare legs are treated as a hazard to be quarantined or insulated.
- **Translator:** Keep a technical verb: *"isolate the legs"* or *"insulate the legs."* It should sound like electrical safety or epidemiology. "Cover them up" loses it.

### 116–117. *большой, массовый квартет*
- **Why it's a meme:** It's a textbook Russian example of an oxymoron and a jab at Soviet gigantomania.
- **Translator:** Translate it literally: *"a big, mass quartet."* Keep both adjectives, because the stacking is part of the joke.

### 123–126. *Даёт она что?… чтоб музыка брала… вела, но не уводила*
- **Why it's a meme:** It parodies the empty directives that cultural officials gave artists.
- **What matters:** *Уводила* means "led astray", which hints at ideological deviation.
- **Translator:** Use the same verb with a twist: *"Music should lead, but not mislead,"* or *"…lead you, but not lead you astray."* The ideological undertone is more important than literal accuracy.

### 127. *А и Б играли на трубе*
- **Why it's funny:** He can't even get a nursery riddle right.
- **Translator:** "Pete and Repeat" is the right genre but has no garbling. A better option is to mangle a well-known English rhyme in the same way, e.g. *"Humpty Dumpty sat on a… played on a horn."* Check the audio first (see loose ends).

### 159. *Нам Гоголи-Щедрины нужны*
- **Why it's a meme:** It echoes Malenkov's 1952 Party Congress line calling for "Soviet Gogols and Shchedrins", which was widely mocked at the time.
- **What matters:** The joke is a man who bans jokes asking for satirists.
- **Translator:** Shchedrin means nothing to English viewers. Use *"We need Gogols and Swifts"*, which keeps a satirist pair. A footnote could add the Malenkov source if needed.

### 164 / 216. *типично / нетипично*
- **What matters:** "The typical" was official Stalin-era aesthetic doctrine (also from the Malenkov speech).
- **Translator:** Use "typical / untypical" every time the word appears. The repetition carries the satire.

### 223–224. *вся соль! — А при чём тут соль?*
- **Why it's funny:** Ogurtsov takes the idiom literally, which shows he can't think in metaphors.
- **Translator:** Rebuild the pun in English: *"That's the whole punchline! — Who's punching?"* or *"That's the point! — What point?"* Accept losing the salt.

### 233 → 426. *петрушка … из директора Петрушку делают*
- **What matters:** The word means both "a muddle" and Petrushka, the Russian Punch puppet. The callback 190 lines later is part of the joke.
- **Translator:** Use one image for both: *"a Punch-and-Judy show"* … *"making a Punch out of the director."*

### 236. *морально разложился*
- **Translator:** Use deadpan party-meeting language for a clown act: *"has morally degenerated."*

### 307. *лектор из общества по распространению*
- **What matters:** The full name was the "Society for the Dissemination of Political and Scientific Knowledge". Cutting it off leaves it absurd.
- **Translator:** *"a lecturer from the Society for the Dissemination."* End the line there.

### 343–362, 511–524. The two songs
- **Why they're memes:** "Five Minutes" is a New Year's Eve standard, and "Good Mood" is quoted for its title.
- **Translator:** Translate for meaning, keeping the line breaks singable. *Без пяти минут мастер* (358) is an idiom for "almost a master". Play it against the five-minute countdown: *"five minutes away from being a master."*

### 376. *Ловкость рук и никакого доклада*
- **What matters:** It twists the magician's patter (*…никакого мошенничества*).
- **Translator:** Use the English patter as the base: *"Sleight of hand, and no report!"* or *"Nothing up my sleeve, and no report!"*

### 393–399. The drunk Mars lecture (the most famous scene)
- **Why it's a meme:** *Есть ли жизнь на Марсе, нет ли жизни на Марсе, это науке неизвестно* is still quoted for any unknowable question. The comedy comes from scientific pomposity collapsing into drunkenness.
- **Translator:**
  - Mars line: keep the repetitive, circular rhythm: *"Is there life on Mars, is there no life on Mars, science does not know."* It should sound quotable.
  - Stars: the slide from star counts to *пять звёздочек* is brandy grading. *"…but best of all, of course, five stars"* works naturally in English.
  - *вооружённым глазом*: English "armed eye" is an archaic astronomy term, so the joke doesn't land. *"With the naked eye… and with the dressed eye"* keeps the absurd flip.
  - *Наука ещё не в курсе дела*: *"science hasn't been briefed yet."* Bureaucratic language applied to science.
  - *Асса!*: keep it as a dance shout, with an optional italic note.

### 420. *Ансамбль пенсии и пляски*
- **Translator:** A pun is required: *"Pension and Dance Ensemble."* It only works if "Song and Dance Ensemble" appears nearby or is clearly implied.

### 422–423. Composer "Kruchini" and the double-barrelled conductor
- **Translator:** Kruchini is fake Italian built on *кручина* ("grief"). Use a fake-Italian English pun, e.g. *"Maestro Lamentini"* or *"Doloroso."*

### 456 / 530. *Буду жаловаться в ВЦСПС*
- **Translator:** The acronym means nothing in English. Use *"I'll complain to the Trade Union Council!"* The pompous institutional threat is what matters.

### 463. *Кассир уехал в банк*
- **Translator:** Use the generic office brush-off: *"Come back later, the cashier's gone to the bank."* This works as is.

### 466–477, 486. The bear fable, *просто называй фамилию и место работы*
- **What matters:** This pays off the bear painting at 94 (probably a nod to Shishkin's famous bear painting). The joke is a request to drop the allegory and name names, which inverts Aesopian criticism.
- **Translator:** Keep the bear imagery consistent from 94 onward, and keep the reply in personnel-form language: *"Just give the surname and place of work."*

### 543–544. *нашли в моём лице дурака*
- **Translator:** The secretary echoing the official phrase is the joke. Use a stiff English formula she can repeat: *"found, in my person, a fool"* → *"In your person, yes."*

### 546–547. *Горько!*
- **What matters:** It's the wedding shout that makes the couple kiss, here turned into "it's bitter to get a year older".
- **Translator:** Two options:
  - Keep *"Gorko!"* with a short in-line gloss, *"(Bitter!—kiss!)"*.
  - Rebuild it around an English kiss cue (mistletoe), losing "bitter".

  I recommend the first, because the bitterness carries the second meaning.

### 561. *Официально заявляю… никакой ответственности не несу*
- **Why it's a meme:** It's quoted as an all-purpose disclaimer.
- **Translator:** Use fully formal legalese, spoken to camera: *"I hereby officially declare… I bear no responsibility whatsoever."*

**Loose ends**
- **Unverified by web, though I'm fairly confident:** the Malenkov 1952 source for "Gogols and Shchedrins" and "the typical", the brandy-star grading, and the Society's full name (it was renamed *Знание* in 1963). A quick check is worth it if any of these go into the paper.
- **Line 127:** check whether the audio says *играли* (his garbling) or *сидели* (a subtitle error).
- **Lines 525/528:** the surname is spelled both *Бурыкина* and *Буракина*. Confirm which is correct.
