=== CHUNK 1 ===
The subtitles are from **_Gentlemen of Fortune_ (_Джентльмены удачи_, 1971)**. Many of its lines are *крылатые фразы*—quotations recognizable outside the film—not necessarily image-based internet memes. The central translation problem is the contrast between a gentle kindergarten director, Troshkin, and the terrifying criminal “Docent” whom he impersonates: Troshkin keeps slipping between nursery-school language, rehearsed underworld slang, and genuine concern for the thieves. ([mosfilm.ru](https://www.mosfilm.ru/cinema/films/dzhentlmeny-udachi/?utm_source=openai))

| Subtitle passage | Why it matters; what to preserve in English |
|---|---|
| **00:02:41; 00:15:19 — «Пасть порву! … моргалы выколю!»** | One of the film’s signature mock-ferocious threats, repeated until it becomes a catchphrase. *Пасть* (“maw”) and *моргалы* (“peepers”) are deliberately rough, not neutral “mouth” and “eyes.” Something like **“I’ll rip your trap open! I’ll poke your peepers out!”** keeps its cartoonish excess. ([vsu.ru](https://www.vsu.ru/ru/university/structure/communicate/pdf/comm-behavior/comm-behavior_2003-1.pdf?utm_source=openai)) |
| **00:07:45 — «Автомашину куплю с магнитофоном, пошью костюм с отливом и в Ялту!»** | A much-quoted fantasy of what the stolen gold will buy: a car with a tape player, a shiny suit, and a holiday in the resort town of Yalta. Its humor lies in the thief’s very specific, modest idea of luxury. Keep those period details rather than substituting contemporary status symbols. ([skio.ru](https://skio.ru/afofilms/kino7.php)) |
| **00:08:53 — «Всё! Кина не будет. Электричество кончилось.»** | A famous dismissal, now reusable when **the anticipated show or plan is off**. *Кина* is colloquial/nonstandard for “cinema”; “That’s it—show’s over. We’re out of electricity” preserves both the brush-off and the absurd explanation. It need not imply a real power failure when quoted elsewhere. ([culture.ru](https://www.culture.ru/live/movies/2704/dzhentlmeny-udachi?utm_source=openai)) |
| **00:11:09–12:16; 00:24:59 — «Так это в Турции, там тепло» / «Как в Турции»** | The professor describes an extravagantly disgusting punishment involving a vat; Troshkin’s practical objection is that **Turkey is warm**. Later, in the cement tanker, “Like in Turkey” recalls that story. Preserve the setup and callback: the second line is not a general observation about travel. ([ru.wikiquote.org](https://ru.wikiquote.org/wiki/%D0%94%D0%B6%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%82%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%8B_%D1%83%D0%B4%D0%B0%D1%87%D0%B8)) |
| **00:12:22–12:56; 00:36:48–37:29 — «Нехороший человек? — Редиска» / «Этот нехороший человек предаст нас…»** | The film’s best-known slang lesson. *Редиска* literally means **“radish”**; here it is a comic insult. Later Troshkin makes the thieves replace their slang with prim, schoolroom Russian, producing the hilariously polite “This bad person will betray us at the first sign of danger.” Don’t translate *редиска* straight into an ordinary insult and erase the radish-to-“bad person” lesson. Much of the film’s supposed criminal vocabulary is playful, rather than a dependable real-world slang glossary. ([ru.wikiquote.org](https://ru.wikiquote.org/wiki/%D0%94%D0%B6%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%82%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%8B_%D1%83%D0%B4%D0%B0%D1%87%D0%B8)) |
| **00:15:19–15:32 — «…сарделька, сосиска, редиска, Навуходоносор, петух гамбургский!»** | Troshkin starts with threats, then runs out of credible insults and improvises a ridiculous list: sausage, frankfurter, radish, Nebuchadnezzar, “Hamburg rooster.” The **collapse from menace into nonsense** is the joke. Preserve the piling-up, not just its purported meaning. ([skio.ru](https://skio.ru/afofilms/kino7.php)) |
| **00:26:49 — «Какой хороший цемент. Не отмывается совсем» / 00:26:49–26:54 — «Все побежали, и я побежал»** | Both are collected as quotable lines. The first praises cement *because* it will not wash off the escaped prisoners; the second is a disarmingly empty explanation for joining an escape. “Everyone ran, so I ran too” can also work as a joke about following a crowd. ([skio.ru](https://skio.ru/afofilms/kino7.php)) |
| **00:30:11–30:19 — «А в тюрьме сейчас ужин… макароны»** | Having escaped, cold and hungry Vasily wistfully remembers the prison dinner. The incongruous nostalgia is the whole line: **“They’ll be having dinner in prison now … pasta.”** Let the pause do some work. It is independently identified as a film quotation. ([dslov.ru](https://dslov.ru/pos/6/p6_318.htm?utm_source=openai)) |
| **00:33:11; 00:34:21 — «Лошадью ходи, лошадью ходи, дурак!»** | A recognizable chess-scene quotation: **“Move your knight, you idiot!”** *Лошадь* is colloquial “horse” for the chess knight; “move your horse” is possible if the translation favors the character’s unpolished voice over standard chess terminology. ([skio.ru](https://skio.ru/afofilms/kino7.php)) |
| **00:35:42–36:18 — «Не играть, не пить, не воровать без меня»** | Troshkin appears to forbid gambling, drinking, and stealing—then adds **“without me.”** Retain that last-minute qualification. In the same scene, “Ali-Baba” turns out to be Vasily’s surname, *Alibabayev*, not a nickname; that matters because Troshkin has just banned nicknames. ([ru.wikiquote.org](https://ru.wikiquote.org/wiki/%D0%94%D0%B6%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%82%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%8B_%D1%83%D0%B4%D0%B0%D1%87%D0%B8)) |
| **00:38:09 — «А Гаврила Петрович по фене ругается!»** | Literally, “Gavrila Petrovich is swearing in thieves’ cant!” A criminal complains like a child telling on a classmate, using a respectful first name and patronymic. *Феня* means criminal argot; a subtitle may need **“He’s using crook talk!”** rather than an unexplained transliteration. ([skio.ru](https://skio.ru/afofilms/kino7.php)) |
| **00:39:44 — «…как три тополя на Плющихе»** | Not just a colorful comparison: it names the earlier film **_Three Poplars on Plyushchikha_**. The three men are conspicuously exposed, and the familiar film title makes a ready-made comparison. “Like three poplars on Plyushchikha Street” keeps the allusion; “sticking out like sore thumbs” conveys the immediate meaning but loses it. ([premier.one](https://premier.one/show/tri-topolya-na-plyuschihe-tsvetnaya-versiya/facts)) |
| **00:43:46–44:08 — «Кто же его посадит? Он же памятник!»** | Among the most famous lines. Asked whether the man depicted by a statue is *sitting*, Kosoy hears *посадить* as **“put in jail”**: “Who’d put him away? He’s a monument!” English needs a deliberate workaround for the sit/jail ambiguity; simply saying “It’s a statue” loses the mechanism. Outside the film the line is also applied ironically to someone treated as untouchable. ([skio.ru](https://skio.ru/afofilms/kino7.php)) |
| **00:46:25 — «Кушать подано! Садитесь жрать, пожалуйста»** | An especially durable quotation. The announcement begins and ends with exaggerated politeness—“Dinner is served … please”—but *жрать* is a coarse “gobble/chow down.” **“Dinner is served! Please sit down and stuff your faces”** keeps the clash of registers. ([vsu.ru](https://www.vsu.ru/ru/university/structure/communicate/pdf/comm-behavior/comm-behavior_2003-1.pdf?utm_source=openai)) |
| **00:48:24–48:32 — «Ты туда не ходи, ты сюда ходи, а то снег башка попадет — совсем мертвый будешь!»** | A famous warning delivered in Vasily’s distinctive broken Russian. He is trying to divert a passerby from the burglary, supposedly for their safety. Grammatical oddity is essential: **“You no go that way. You go this way. Snow hit head—you be completely dead!”** Avoid turning his speech into a generic accent unrelated to the words. ([culture.ru](https://www.culture.ru/live/movies/2704/dzhentlmeny-udachi?utm_source=openai)) |
| **00:49:34 — «Вежливость — лучшее оружие вора»** | Troshkin’s paradoxical criminal maxim: being courteous helps the gang gain access without attracting suspicion. “**Politeness is a thief’s best weapon**” is both close and quotable. ([ru.wikiquote.org](https://ru.wikiquote.org/wiki/%D0%94%D0%B6%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%82%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%8B_%D1%83%D0%B4%D0%B0%D1%87%D0%B8)) |
| **00:59:47–59:58 — «Украл, выпил — в тюрьму. … Романтика!»** | Perhaps the most portable line. Troshkin mockingly sells the thief’s repetitive, miserable life as adventure: **“Steal, drink, go to jail. Steal, drink, go to jail. Romance!”** The final word is ironic, *not* sincere praise for crime. It remains in use as shorthand for a self-defeating cycle. ([rutube.ru](https://rutube.ru/video/ae74e2dacc5eaf588393cf312f3c9f29/?utm_source=openai)) |
| **01:10:49–11:20 — «Я обязательно вас найду»** | Troshkin intends a warm New Year’s promise: **“I’ll find you, wherever you are.”** The thieves hear the same words as a threat from Docent to hunt them down. Translate the sentence plainly enough to support **both** readings; explaining his good intentions in the subtitle would spoil the next exchange. ([ru.wikiquote.org](https://ru.wikiquote.org/wiki/%D0%94%D0%B6%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%82%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%8B_%D1%83%D0%B4%D0%B0%D1%87%D0%B8)) |
| **01:13:32–13:50 — «Воркута» / «Джамбул… там тепло, там мой дом, там моя мама»** | In a cities word game, one man names **Vorkuta** because he served time there; Vasily offers **Dzhambul** regardless of the required letter, because it is home and his mother is there. This is less a meme than a culturally important tonal turn. Don’t silently “correct” Vasily’s wrong answer: it reveals what matters to him. ([ru.wikiquote.org](https://ru.wikiquote.org/wiki/%D0%94%D0%B6%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%82%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%8B_%D1%83%D0%B4%D0%B0%D1%87%D0%B8)) |

**Two other references worth flagging:** At **01:04:51–05:04**, “Yes, yes, *OBKhSS*” is a sound-play on English *yes* and the initials of a Soviet police department; an English-only “yes, yes” loses the abrupt slide back into Soviet life. At **00:57:02–57:20**, “Проснись и пой” (“Wake up and sing”) is a recognizable song heard in the film, **not dialogue coined by it**; treat it as song lyrics, not another invented criminal catchphrase. ([ru.wikiquote.org](https://ru.wikiquote.org/wiki/%D0%94%D0%B6%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%82%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%8B_%D1%83%D0%B4%D0%B0%D1%87%D0%B8))
