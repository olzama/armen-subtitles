=== CHUNK 1 ===
This is **Gentlemen of Fortune** (*Джентльмены удачи*, 1971). Aleksandr Sery directed it, and Georgy Daneliya and Viktoria Tokareva wrote the script. It is one of the most quoted Soviet comedies, and a Russian roundup of "50 most famous phrases" covers almost every line below. Most of the cultural context here comes from my own knowledge of the film. The searches only confirmed which lines are famous and that stills from the film are still used as meme templates.

## Top-tier catchphrases

| Line (sub #) | What's going on | Translator notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Пасть порву! / моргалы выколю / рога поотшибаю** (3, 93, 406…) | Docent's standard threats. The joke is that he says them every time. Troshkin's fake-gangster version piles them up and adds his own "носы пооткушу" ("I'll bite your noses off"). | Use one fixed English form every time, e.g. "I'll rip your jaw off" and "I'll gouge your peepers out." Fedya's complaint "Только это и знаешь" (391) and "Пасть, пасть…" (485, 536) only work if the threat is the same each time. |
| **Редиска** = нехороший человек (73, 240, 359–360, 508) | Prison slang ("radish": red outside, white inside, so two-faced) that became general Russian. The film explains it itself in the glossary scene (240–243), and "Ответь за редиску!" ("Take back the radish!") is a joke in its own right. | Keep it literal ("radish"). The in-film definition carries the meaning, and the glossary scene needs a word that means nothing to outsiders. |
| **Сарделька, сосиска, редиска, Навуходоносор, петух гамбургский!** (93–95) | Troshkin is a kindergarten head bluffing like a hardened criminal. He strings together harmless nonsense insults, and it still works. | What matters is that it sounds absurd and rhythmic, not what the words mean: "you wiener, you sausage, you radish, Nebuchadnezzar, you Hamburg rooster!" Keep the escalation. |
| **Кто ж его посадит? Он же памятник!** (291–293) | Pun on *сидячий/посадить*: "seated" (a sitting statue) vs. "put in jail." This is the film's most quoted line. | You have to rebuild the pun. One option: "Is he sitting?" … "Who'd put him away? He's a statue!" Or use "doing time" / "sitting statue." |
| **Ты туда не ходи, ты сюда ходи, а то снег башка попадёт — совсем мёртвый будешь** (324–326) | Ali-Baba's broken Russian, said kindly. Fedya repeats it later to an old woman. Still a standard way to give a warning jokingly. | Write it in consistent broken English, e.g. "You no go there, you go here, or snow fall on head, you be totally dead." Fedya's repeat (326) should copy it exactly. |
| **— Таня. — А меня Федя. — Ну и дура.** (345–346) | A pick-up line that ends in a non sequitur. Very famous. | Keep the flat, deadpan timing. Literal works: "I'm Fedya." / "Then you're a fool." |
| **Кушать подано! Садитесь жрать, пожалуйста** (307, 394) | Clash of registers. *Кушать подано* is the stock butler's line from theatre ("Dinner is served"), while *жрать* is crude ("stuff your faces"). | "Dinner is served. Sit down and stuff your faces, please." |
| **Украл, выпил — в тюрьму. Романтика!** / **Джентльмен удачи** (383–384) | Where the title comes from. *Джентльмен удачи* is the pirate euphemism from *Treasure Island*, so English has a matching phrase. | "Gentleman of fortune" works directly. "Steal, drink, go to jail. Romance!" is a well-known formula, so keep it short and repeated. |
| **Всё! Кина не будет! Электричество кончилось** (43) | *Кина* is a deliberately wrong form (*кино* doesn't decline). It's now an idiom meaning "show's over, nothing's happening." | Mark it as uneducated speech: "No movie today, the 'lectricity's run out." |
| **Как три тополя на Плющихе** (256) | Refers to the 1967 film *Three Poplars on Plyushchikha*. Means "sticking out where everyone can see us." | The reference won't land. Use "sticking out like a sore thumb," or keep the poplars if you add a note. |
| **Автомашину куплю с магнитофоном, пошью костюм с отливом — и в Ялту!** (36) | A petty crook's Soviet idea of luxury: a car with a tape player, a shiny suit, and a holiday at the Black Sea resort of Yalta. | A short gloss like "a sharkskin suit" or "a shiny suit" is enough. |
| **Йес, йес, ОБХСС** (411) | Pun: "yes" rhymes with ОБХСС, the Soviet police unit that dealt with theft of state property and black-market dealing. **The subtitle has a typo: it should be ОБХСС, not ОБХС.** | Hard to carry over. You could rhyme "yes" with something police-related, e.g. "Yes, yes, the CID… the Fraud Squad!" |

## Strong secondary lines

- **Чуть что — сразу Косой / Федя** (110, 484, 546): a running "always blame me" catchphrase. Translate it the same way every time.
- **Как в Турции** (153): a callback to the colonel's story about the Turkish vat (65–69). It only works if the setup is translated clearly.
- **— А что, «Динамо» бежит? — Все бегут** (182): Dynamo was the sports society of the police and security services, so "is Dynamo running?" also means "are the cops after us?" Trudovye Rezervy ("Labour Reserves") was the society for vocational schools. A footnote may be the only option. Otherwise "Everyone's running" still works as a joke about escaped convicts.
- **Все побежали — и я побежал** (162): used as a meme about following the crowd.
- **Я говорил тебе… Ну, вот и не обижайся!** (300–303): a classic setup with a three-part build-up. Keep the repetition.
- **Ходи лошадью!** (212, 216): an annoying kibitzer using the street name for the knight ("horse"). "Move the horse!" keeps the lack of polish.
- **Женские туфли хочу. Три штуки. Размер 42, 43, 45** (342): absurd. *Три штуки* ("three pieces") is the joke, so render it as "three items" or "three each."
- **Финита ля комедия** (528): mangled Italian that was already a Russian catchphrase from Lermontov. Keep it in Italian.
- **Деточка, а вам не кажется, что ваше место возле параши?** (99): an insult based on prison pecking order. *Параша* is the toilet bucket in the cell, where the lowest-ranked inmates sleep. "Your bunk is by the slop bucket" gets the hierarchy across.
- **Деньги ваши — будут наши** (137), **Чем больше сдадим — тем лучше** (556), **Я не прокурор, чтоб с тобой по душам разговаривать** (472), **Мы будем прятать, а он дырка смотреть?** (482, Ali-Baba's broken Russian again), **Доцент бы заставил** (416), **Обрили уже!** (558, the final punchline), and **Ухи, ухи!** (221) are all widely quoted.
- **Летчик-налетчик** (504): a rhyme pun, "test pilot / stick-up man." Something like "a jet pilot? A jail pilot" keeps the rhyme.

## Running jokes that span the film

- **"Доцент"** is an academic rank (associate professor) used as a criminal nickname. The film plays on it: "Я — Доцент! — Поздравляем!" (191, "I'm the Docent!" / "Congratulations!"), "— Профессор не приехал? — Нет, Доцент приехал" (423), and the archaeologists' cover story. The name needs to read as an academic title. Options are "the Docent" or "the Professor," but then 423 needs "Assistant Professor."
- **The prison-slang glossary** (70–74, 239–243): канать (scram), фуфло толкать (lie), тики-так (OK), тошниловка (beer joint, literally "puke-house"), гоп-стоп (robbery). The joke is Troshkin learning crook-speak like a foreign language. Pick one invented-sounding slang word for each and reuse it, because "тики-так" comes back at 6 and 86.
- **Шапка / шапочка for the golden helmet** (8, 534): first "Тяжёлая шапочка" ("Heavy little cap") and later Ali-Baba's "жуликов много, а шапка одна" ("crooks are many, but there's only one hat").
- **Children's songs:** "Нам не страшен серый волк" (44) is the Russian version of the Disney song "Who's Afraid of the Big Bad Wolf?". "В лесу родилась ёлочка" (313) is the standard Russian New Year carol. Gangsters singing a kindergarten song is the joke. Use the English Disney song for 44 and a generic carol or a gloss for 313.
- **Может, козла забьём?** (188): "Fancy a game of dominoes?" It's a working-class idiom, not about goats.

## Problems in the subtitle file itself

- 411 should read ОБХ**СС**, and 197 "Вр оде" should be "Вроде". Numbers are broken by OCR spacing: "1 00" (131), "1 0-00" (135), "1 9 рублей" (200), "1 4" (507), "1 88" (170).
- 75 "Фрей-фея" looks garbled. The scene follows "Хороший человек? — Забыл!", and the answer is probably a slang form of "фраер." Check it against the audio before translating.
