# Meme Translation Guide: *Gentlemen of Fortune* (1971) → English

**Global rule:** Most of these lines are funny because they **repeat** or because they **clash in register**. Fix one English form for each catchphrase and reuse it exactly. Consistency matters more than how good any single rendering is.

## Top-tier catchphrases

**Пасть порву / моргалы выколю / рога поотшибаю** (3, 93, 406…)
- **Why it's a meme:** Docent's threats are formulaic, and the formula is the joke. Fedya's "Только это и знаешь" (391) and "Пасть, пасть…" (485, 536) mock the repetition directly.
- **Translator target:** Use crude, physical, slightly archaic thug-speak, and keep it identical every time: "I'll rip your jaw off," "I'll gouge your peepers out," "I'll knock your horns off." Troshkin's add-on "носы пооткушу" should feel like an amateur overdoing it: "I'll bite your noses off!"

**Редиска** (73, 240–243, 359–360, 508)
- **Why it's a meme:** It's a soft-sounding vegetable used as a serious criminal insult (red outside, white inside, so two-faced). The film defines it on screen, and "Ответь за редиску!" turns the word into a matter of honour.
- **Translator target:** Keep "radish." The glossary scene supplies the meaning, and the absurdity of demanding satisfaction over a vegetable is the payoff.

**Сарделька, сосиска, редиска, Навуходоносор, петух гамбургский!** (93–95)
- **Why it's a meme:** A kindergarten director bluffs his way through a prison confrontation with a string of nonsense, and it works.
- **Missing from the findings:** *петух* is also the harshest prison slur (lowest caste, a passive homosexual). So the list ends on the one real insult, which Troshkin uses without knowing it. That is why it lands with the inmates.
- **Translator target:** Keep the rhythm and the escalation from harmless to cutting: "You wiener, you sausage, you radish, Nebuchadnezzar, you Hamburg cock!" "Cock" gives a mild double edge. A fully explicit slur would break the innocence of the joke.

**Кто ж его посадит? Он же памятник!** (291–293)
- **Why it's a meme:** It's a pun on *сидячий* ("seated") and *посадить* ("to jail"). It's the most quoted line in the film.
- **Translator target:** Rebuild the pun in English. Literal "sit" does not mean prison in English. A workable option uses "inside": "Is he inside?" / "How could he be inside? He's a statue!" The flat logic of the reply has to stay deadpan.

**Ты туда не ходи, ты сюда ходи, а то снег башка попадёт — совсем мёртвый будешь** (324–326)
- **Why it's a meme:** Broken Russian used to give a kindly, absurdly literal warning. Russians use it as a joking "careful!"
- **Translator target:** Write consistent pidgin that sounds warm, not mocking: "You no go there, you go here, or snow fall on head, you be totally dead." Any repeat must match it word for word.
- **Note:** Check who actually speaks this line. I'm not confident it's Ali-Baba.

**— Таня. — А меня Федя. — Ну и дура.** (345–346)
- **Why it's a meme:** A clumsy pick-up attempt collapses into a non sequitur insult.
- **Translator target:** Keep it flat and unexplained: "Well, you're an idiot." The findings' "*Then* you're a fool" adds a causal logic that isn't in the original and weakens the absurdity.
- **Note:** Check speaker attribution, since *дура* is feminine.

**Кушать подано! Садитесь жрать, пожалуйста** (307, 394)
- **Why it's a meme:** A theatrical butler formula is followed by a crude verb.
- **Translator target:** Keep the jolt between registers: "Dinner is served. Sit down and stuff your faces, please." Keep "please," because the politeness makes the crudeness funnier.

**Украл, выпил — в тюрьму. Романтика! / Джентльмен удачи** (383–384)
- **Why it's a meme:** It deflates criminal romance into three verbs. The title phrase comes from the pirate euphemism in Stevenson.
- **Translator target:** "Gentleman of fortune" exists in English, so use it as is. For the formula, use a terse staccato: "Steal, drink, jail. Romance!"

**Всё! Кина не будет! Электричество кончилось** (43)
- **Why it's a meme:** The illiterate form *кина* is now a stock way to say "show's over."
- **Translator target:** Mark the speech as uneducated and make it sound final and idiomatic: "That's it! No pictures tonight! 'Lectricity's run out."

**Как три тополя на Плющихе** (256)
- **Why it's a meme:** It references a 1967 film.
- **Translator target:** The reference won't reach an English audience. Use "sticking out like a sore thumb." Keep the poplars only in a scholarly or annotated edition.

**Автомашину куплю с магнитофоном, пошью костюм с отливом — и в Ялту!** (36)
- **Why it's a meme:** It captures a petty crook's provincial idea of luxury.
- **Translator target:** Make the dream specific, a bit tacky, and of its era: "a car with a tape deck, a shiny sharkskin suit, and off to Yalta!" Keep Yalta, since the context makes clear it's a resort.

**Йес, йес, ОБХСС** (411)
- **Why it's a meme:** A rhyme joke that ties English "yes" to the Soviet economic-crimes police.
- **Translator target:** The rhyme is the joke, so it has to rhyme. The findings' "CID… Fraud Squad" doesn't. A better option is "Yes, yes — IRS!", which rhymes and keeps the sense of a fraud or finance authority. It does shift the line to an American setting, so decide on that domestication once for the whole film.

## Secondary lines

- **Чуть что — сразу Косой / Федя** (110, 484, 546): a running "always blame me" line. Use one fixed form: "Anything happens, it's always Kosoy!"
- **Как в Турции** (153): a callback that only works if 65–69 (the Turkish vat story) is translated clearly. Use the same key word in both places.
- **«Динамо» бежит? — Все бегут** (182): the sports-society and police subtext won't carry over. Make sure "Everyone's running" lands as a joke about escaped convicts. Use a footnote only in an annotated edition.
- **Все побежали — и я побежал** (162): a meme about following the crowd. Keep it simple and childlike: "Everyone ran, so I ran."
- **Я говорил тебе… Ну, вот и не обижайся!** (300–303): keep the three-step build-up word for word.
- **Ходи лошадью!** (212, 216): "Move the horse!" keeps the kibitzer's lack of polish. Don't correct it to "knight."
- **Женские туфли… Три штуки. 42, 43, 45** (342): "Three items" or "three pairs, sizes…" The bureaucratic counting word is the joke.
- **Финита ля комедия** (528): keep the mangled Italian, "Finita la comedia." English audiences will recognise it too.
- **Деточка… возле параши** (99): the condescending "sweetie" combined with the threat about prison hierarchy is the point. "Sweetie, don't you think your bunk's by the slop bucket?"
- **Летчик-налетчик** (504): the rhyme matters more than precision. One option is "Not a pilot — a pirate!"
- **Обрили уже!** (558): the final punchline. Keep it short and resigned: "Already shaved!"

## Running jokes

- **Доцент:** the name must read as an academic title. "The Docent" is opaque to English audiences. "The Professor" is clearer but needs "Assistant Professor" at 423. Choose one before translating 191 and 423.
- **Prison-slang glossary** (70–74, 239–243): Troshkin learns crook-speak like a foreign language. Coin or choose one slang word for each term and reuse it. *Тики-так* comes back at 6 and 86.
- **Шапочка / шапка** (8, 534): use "cap" or "hat" both times so Ali-Baba's "many crooks, only one hat" echoes the earlier line.
- **Children's songs:** for 44, use "Who's Afraid of the Big Bad Wolf?" For 313, use a well-known children's carol. Gangsters singing nursery songs is the joke.
- **Козла забьём?** (188): "Fancy a game of dominoes?" It's not about goats.
